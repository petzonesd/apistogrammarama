Sourcing Quality Fish

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Short Version
  • Specialist breeders and importers beat general retail for a confirmed species/strain
  • Ask about locality and lineage, especially for macmasteri/viejita/steindachneri
  • Look for clean fins, active behavior, and steady color
  • Quarantine every new fish for 2–4 weeks before adding to your main tank

Where to Buy

Apistogramma shows up through a few different channels, each with different tradeoffs:

  • Specialty aquarium stores — like Pet Zone Tropical Fish — typically carry the more commonly available species (cacatuoides, borellii) and staff who can speak to care and sourcing. Good for a first apisto.
  • Dedicated apisto and cichlid breeders — the best source once you want a specific, confidently-identified species, strain, or locality. More common at aquarium society auctions and swaps, or found through the online communities on our community page.
  • Importers — for wild-caught, locality-specific fish. Generally the route for advanced keepers working on a breeding project tied to a specific wild population, since wild imports are often less parameter-flexible than domestic-bred lines.
  • General big-box pet retailers — occasionally carry apistos, but species identification and health history are less reliable here than through a specialist. Fine in a pinch, worth extra scrutiny before buying.

What to Look For

Whatever the source, the same basic health checks apply:

  • Fins: Clean and undamaged, or at least clearly healing rather than actively deteriorating. Ragged, receding fin edges can indicate fin rot.
  • Behavior: Active and curious rather than listless or hiding constantly. A healthy apisto investigates you back at the glass.
  • Color: Steady, not washed-out or unusually pale — though keep in mind stress coloring during transport and a new-tank adjustment period is normal and temporary.
  • Body condition: No visible bloating, sunken belly despite eating, or spinal deformity.
  • Tankmates: Check the health of other fish sharing the seller's tank — a sick tankmate is a real warning sign even if the individual fish you're buying looks fine.

A Note on Mislabeling

This genus has a real, persistent mislabeling problem, especially among the closely related macmasteri / viejita / steindachneri cluster — species that are genuinely difficult to distinguish by eye, particularly as females, and are frequently sold under whichever name is more marketable. If a confirmed species matters to you — for a breeding project or simply wanting to know what's in your tank — buy from a source that can speak to locality or lineage, not a general retailer using a name as a catch-all. See the individual species pages for the specific lookalike issues that affect each fish.

Questions Worth Asking a Seller

  • Is this fish tank-bred or wild-caught, and if wild, what locality?
  • What water parameters was it raised in?
  • How long has it been in your care, and has it been eating well?
  • Can I see the parent fish or breeding stock, if tank-bred?

A seller who can answer these confidently is generally a more reliable source than one who can't — it's a reasonable proxy for how seriously they track their stock.

Quarantine Before Adding to Your Main Tank

Every new apisto — regardless of source — should spend two to four weeks in a separate quarantine tank before joining an established display. This protects your existing fish from anything the new arrival might be carrying, gives the new fish time to settle without the added stress of established tankmates, and gives you a controlled space to treat anything that shows up before it can spread. See our common diseases guide for what to watch for during quarantine.

Acclimating a New Fish

Drip acclimate over 45–60 minutes minimum, longer for wild imports or fish coming from noticeably different water than your own. This matters more for apistos than for hardier community fish — see our water parameters guide for why a gradual introduction genuinely reduces stress and improves survival for this genus.
