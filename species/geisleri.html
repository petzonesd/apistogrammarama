Apistogramma geisleri

Apistogramma geisleri

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 2.8cm (1.1") SL max per FishBase/Kullander (2003) — among the smallest in the genus; hobbyist accounts disagree with some written claims of a "fairly large" fish
  • Tank: No species-specific minimum documented; genus-standard footprint with a visually complex layout, not an open one
  • pH: Neutral to slightly acidic (no numeric range published)
  • Hardness: Moderately soft water (no numeric range published)
  • Temperature: Not documented species-specifically; genus-standard 73–82°F used as a working default
  • Temperament: Reportedly peaceful and undemanding; males show brief dark coloration while sparring
  • Difficulty: Advanced (genuine trade rarity + a documented identification mix-up with other species on this site)

Overview

Apistogramma geisleri is a small, genuinely rare lower-Amazon dwarf cichlid whose own identity has been disputed in the trade more than once. Heiko Meinken formally described it in 1971, in the German journal Senckenbergiana Biologica (volume 52, issues 1–2, page 35), from a holotype (SMF 10617) and two paratypes (SMF 10618–19), all held at Frankfurt's Senckenberg Museum and collected from the Rio Curuçamba near Óbidos, Pará, Brazil — in the lower Amazon basin, close to where the Trombetas River joins it. Sven Kullander, the taxonomist behind so much of this genus's modern classification, folded geisleri into his own broader 1980 revision of the group.

The species' common name, Geisler's dwarf cichlid, honors Dr. Rolf Geisler (1925–2012) — a German aquarist, limnologist, and ichthyologist who held a professorship at the University of Freiburg. That puts geisleri in good company on this site: honorific species names already cover hobby pioneer Hermann Meinken (this site's own meinkeni), museum curator Karl-Heinz Lüling (luelingi), and ichthyologist Charles Tate Regan (regani), among others.

Natural Habitat

FishBase lists geisleri's distribution as the Amazon River basin, specifically the lower Trombetas River region — consistent with the Óbidos-area type locality from the original 1971 description. No source checked for this guide documents a wider confirmed range beyond this lower-Amazon region. The species lives in freshwater, benthopelagic habitat within a tropical 21–29°C band, per FishBase.

Temperament & Compatibility

A longtime keeper's documented account describes geisleri as undemanding and otherwise unremarkable in temperament, aside from sparring males briefly showing dark coloration when agitated — consistent with the species being described elsewhere as peaceful. No source checked for this guide documents specific tankmate recommendations beyond the general genus standard of avoiding aggressive or fin-nipping species.

A Documented Identification Mix-Up

Geisleri has a real, forum-documented history of being confused with other species in this corner of the genus — including two already covered on this site. A detailed apistogramma.com thread traces one specific import: fish were originally sold on Aquabid under the placeholder name "cf. taeniata/cf. reticulosa 'Careiro'" — "reticulosa" here being a hobbyist spelling of this site's own A. resticulosa, not a distinct species, and "cf. taeniata" referencing this site's own A. taeniata, the genus's formal type species. Identification specialist Frank Hättich reviewed photos of the fish and argued they more plausibly belonged to the A. regani species-complex, with traits similar to true geisleri, rather than to either taeniata or resticulosa. He recommended the working name "A. cf. geisleri (Careiro)" instead — noting that Careiro, the collection point tied to that import, sits roughly 100km from geisleri's own type locality near Óbidos, which is itself a reason exporters sometimes name a fish after a convenient shipping point rather than where it was actually caught.

The practical lesson for buyers: a trade name anywhere near taeniata, resticulosa, or geisleri in this part of the genus is frequently a working guess rather than a confirmed identification, and even experienced identifiers can only narrow it to a species-complex rather than a single name from photos alone.

Breeding

Documented hobbyist experience describes geisleri as breeding readily in the typical Apistogramma manner once given adequate food, water quality, and habitat complexity — one keeper's account on DwarfCichlid.com describes bred lines carried across six or seven generations. A female in brood care reportedly displays a row of spots along her side, which the same account describes as a signal she uses to communicate with both her fry and the attending male. No source checked for this guide documents a specific egg count, hatch time, or fry-growth timeline for this species. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

This is a genuine rarity, and the documented history explains why. Geisleri first reached the German hobby in 1967 — four years before its own formal description — then largely vanished from trade, with only occasional reintroductions through the 1980s and 1990s, and it remains rarely seen in the American hobby today. The most recent documented wild shipment comes from German wholesale exporter Aquarium Glaser, which picked up geisleri as bycatch in December 2015 while collecting wild A. agassizii "Tefé II" — the exporter's own write-up calls it one of the rarest apistogrammas in the hobby, describing it as a lively fish with fast, striking color-pattern changes despite a fairly drab base coloration. Combined with the documented identification mix-up above, budget for a long wait through a specialist importer or club, and don't assume a "geisleri" label on a general retail tank is correctly identified. See our general sourcing guide for broader guidance on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Geisleri is a quietly interesting case study in how trade names drift in this genus: a fish first imported before it even had a scientific name, later confused on the open market with two species already covered on this site, and still rare enough today that a 2015 wholesale shipment calling it bycatch was treated as a notable find. If you want to research the species it's most often mixed up with, this site's own taeniata and resticulosa pages cover both sides of that documented confusion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who was Apistogramma geisleri named after?

Dr. Rolf Geisler (1925–2012), a German aquarist, limnologist, and ichthyologist who was a professor at the University of Freiburg. Heiko Meinken formally described the species in 1971 in the journal Senckenbergiana Biologica (volume 52, issues 1–2, page 35), based on a holotype (cataloged SMF 10617) and two paratypes (SMF 10618–19) collected from the Rio Curuçamba near Óbidos, Pará, Brazil — in the lower Amazon near where the Trombetas River meets it. Sven Kullander folded the species into his own broader taxonomic work on the genus in 1980.

Why do some fish get sold as "cf. geisleri (Careiro)" instead of plain geisleri?

Because a documented identification dispute on the apistogramma.com forum found that fish from a specific import didn't match true geisleri cleanly. Those fish were originally sold on Aquabid as "cf. taeniata/cf. reticulosa 'Careiro'" — reticulosa here being a hobbyist spelling of this site's own A. resticulosa, not a separate species. Identification specialist Frank Hättich argued the fish more likely belonged to the A. regani species-complex, with traits similar to true geisleri, and recommended the working name "A. cf. geisleri (Careiro)" instead — since the Careiro collection point sits roughly 100km from geisleri's actual type locality near Óbidos. It's a useful reminder that a name on a bag or auction listing in this corner of the genus is often a best guess, not a certainty.

How big does Apistogramma geisleri get, and why do accounts disagree?

FishBase, citing Kullander's 2003 genus checklist, lists a maximum of 2.8cm standard length for males or unsexed individuals — among the smallest fish in an already small-bodied genus. But a longtime keeper's detailed account on DwarfCichlid.com reports that his own geisleri were among the smallest Apistogramma he'd kept across decades in the hobby, and notes this conflicts with some written accounts that describe the species as fairly large. No source checked for this guide resolves that conflict, so treat 2.8cm SL as the documented scientific maximum and expect some disagreement if you compare notes with other keepers.

What water parameters and tank setup does Apistogramma geisleri need?

Documented hobbyist guidance is fairly general rather than precisely numeric: neutral to slightly acidic pH and moderately soft water, over a sand substrate, in a tank with a visually complex layout rather than an open one. No source checked for this guide publishes specific pH, hardness, or temperature figures for this species. See our general water parameters guide and tank setup guide for the genus-standard defaults to start from until more species-specific data turns up.

How available is Apistogramma geisleri in the hobby today, and is it endangered?

Genuinely scarce, but not for conservation reasons. The species was first imported into the German hobby in 1967, four years before Meinken's formal 1971 description, then largely disappeared from the trade with only sporadic reintroductions through the 1980s and 1990s; it's rarely seen in the American hobby today. A December 2015 shipment from German wholesale exporter Aquarium Glaser picked up wild geisleri as bycatch while collecting A. agassizii "Tefé II" — the exporter's own write-up calls it one of the rarest apistogrammas in the hobby. Its IUCN Red List status, by contrast, is Least Concern (assessed 2018): the rarity is about who's collecting and exporting it, not population risk.
