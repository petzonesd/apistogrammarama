Apistogramma viejita

Viejita Apisto

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

Before You Buy: Read This

True Apistogramma viejita is genuinely rare in the hobby. Most fish sold under this name — even in good faith — are A. macmasteri or a hybrid between the two. The species are extremely difficult to tell apart, especially as females, and misidentification happens even among experienced keepers. If you want the real thing, buy from a specialist who can speak to locality, not a general retailer.

Overview

Apistogramma viejita is a rare, red-shouldered South American dwarf cichlid so frequently confused with the much more common A. macmasteri that most fish sold under its name are actually the lookalike species. It's a striking dwarf cichlid from the Orinoco River basin, closely related to — and frequently confused with — macmasteri and steindachneri. Males show a red-shouldered pattern with extended dorsal and anal fins and considerably more vibrant coloration than females, who are smaller, plainer, and among the hardest apisto females to confidently identify by sight.

Because so much of the trade supply is actually macmasteri sold under the more evocative "viejita" name, this page covers what genuine viejita needs — which tracks closely with its lookalikes — and, just as importantly, how to think about what you're actually bringing home.

The Essentials
  • Size: Male to about 2.8" (7 cm), female smaller
  • Tank: 20+ gallons
  • pH: 5–7
  • Temperature: 75–84°F (24–29°C)
  • Hardness: Soft, up to 8 dGH
  • Temperament: Semi-aggressive; males intolerant of each other
  • Difficulty: Advanced (largely due to sourcing, not care)

Tank Setup

Viejita is a committed substrate-sifter — sand is not optional here; coarse gravel risks gill damage as the fish works through it constantly for food. Beyond that, setup mirrors macmasteri and steindachneri: driftwood, rock structure, dense low planting, and leaf litter for a soft, tannin-tinted blackwater feel. Keep flow gentle and lighting dim to moderate.

Feeding

An unfussy omnivore that takes flake, pellet, and live or frozen foods without issue. As with most apistos, live foods measurably improve color and breeding readiness.

Temperament & Compatibility

Territorial and semi-aggressive, with males firmly intolerant of other males in anything short of a large, well-structured tank. Keep a single male with one or a small number of females. Compatible with peaceful community fish that don't compete for bottom territory; not a safe tankmate for dwarf shrimp once mature.

Breeding

Cave spawning, female-led guarding, male perimeter defense — the standard apisto pattern. Soft, acidic water supports more reliable spawning, though this species (like macmasteri) shows some tolerance for less-than-perfect parameters if the fish are domestically bred and acclimated. Fry care follows the usual sequence: infusoria or fine fry food first, then newly hatched brine shrimp as they grow, with consistent water changes throughout.

Buying With Confidence

If a confirmed species matters to you — for a breeding project, a biotope build, or simply wanting to know what's actually in your tank — buy from an importer or specialist breeder who can trace locality, rather than a general aquarium store using "viejita" as a catch-all label for red-shouldered apistos. If you already own fish sold as viejita and aren't certain, treat them as you would macmasteri; the care overlaps almost completely, and the two hybridize readily, which is part of why the confusion persists.

Final Thoughts

Viejita is less a distinct care challenge than a sourcing one — the fish itself isn't meaningfully harder to keep than macmasteri, but knowing what you actually have takes real diligence. Approach it as a collector's project: chase the confirmed locality if that's what you want, and don't be discouraged if what you end up with is a beautiful macmasteri instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the viejita I bought actually a viejita?

Probably not, unless it came from a specialist. Most fish sold under the name viejita — even in good faith — are actually Apistogramma macmasteri or a hybrid between the two.

How can I tell viejita apart from macmasteri?

It's extremely difficult, especially in females. The species are frequently confused even by experienced keepers, so a confident ID generally requires buying from a specialist who can speak to locality.

What tank size and water does viejita need?

20 or more gallons, sand substrate for sifting, and soft, acidic water — setup closely mirrors macmasteri and steindachneri.

Is viejita hard to keep?

Not particularly — its care needs are nearly identical to macmasteri. The real difficulty is sourcing a confidently identified fish, not day-to-day husbandry.

Can viejita and macmasteri hybridize?

Yes, they hybridize readily, which is part of why the confusion between the two species persists in the trade.
