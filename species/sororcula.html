Apistogramma sororcula

Apistogramma sororcula

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: up to 4.2cm (1.7") SL — FishBase; holotype male 40.1mm SL
  • Tank: not documented; by analogy to this site's similarly-sized staecki and linkei, 20g long (60–100cm) is a safe default
  • pH: 6.2–6.7 at the type locality (original description)
  • Temperature: 75–82°F (24–28°C), type locality
  • Hardness: <1°dH total/temporary, 5–20 µS/cm conductivity — genuinely very soft water
  • Temperament: Polygynous cave spawner in aquaria; not otherwise documented
  • Difficulty: Advanced — thin trade documentation under its own valid name

Overview

Apistogramma sororcula is a small Bolivian-Brazilian dwarf cichlid whose Latin name says exactly what it is: "little sister." Wolfgang Staeck and Ingo Schindler described it in 2016 (Vertebrate Zoology 66(2): 141–150), and FishBase's etymology note confirms the name was chosen specifically to reflect the species' close similarity to and relationship with this site's own A. staecki — the fish Staeck himself was honored with back in 1985. The original description backs the family resemblance with specific diagnostic differences: sororcula's male caudal fin carries 10 to 12 (occasionally 13) vertical bars against up to 8 in staecki, its lateral band runs continuously to the caudal spot where staecki's does not, every dorsal-fin lappet is produced rather than just lappets 4 through 6, and males carry a dark chin marking absent from the staecki material the authors examined.

Wolfgang Staeck's own history with this fish runs deeper than just the naming. This site's staecki page already notes that Staeck returned to the same river system in 2008 as a taxonomist, co-describing this site's A. erythrura with the same partner, Ingo Schindler, who joined him again for sororcula eight years later — and that he separately described this site's own A. velifera solo in 2003. Sororcula makes a fourth Staeck-connected species on this site, and the first one he named explicitly for its resemblance to his own namesake fish.

The scientific description, though, arrived decades after the hobby already knew the fish existed. Amazonas Magazine's coverage of the 2016 paper places its hobby discovery in 1987, when it began circulating under provisional trade names including "Apistogramma cf. staecki 'Rio Guaporé'" and "A. sp. Guaporé" — a 29-year gap between aquarists first encountering the fish and taxonomists giving it a validated name of its own.

Natural Habitat

The holotype (MTD-F 33580, an adult male 40.1mm SL collected by Staeck himself in 2004) comes from Bolivia's Departamento Beni, in the río San Martín about 5km upstream of the village Bella Vista — part of the broader Rio Guaporé (Iténez) drainage spanning Bolivia and Brazil, the same system this site's own staecki inhabits. Amazonas Magazine notes that this site's A. trifasciata shares that drainage too, alongside a third species, A. inconspicua, not yet covered on this site.

The original description gives genuinely precise field data: the species lives in lentic, shallow littoral zones of lagoons and rivers at roughly 5 to 40cm depth, concentrated wherever thick layers of dead leaves or aquatic and submerged vegetation provide cover — abundance tracks available cover directly. It tolerates clearwater, whitewater, or mixed conditions. Recorded water chemistry at the type locality: pH 6.2 to 6.7, total and temporary hardness under 1°dH, conductivity 5 to 20 µS/cm, and temperature 24 to 28°C (75–82°F). The río San Martín's water level swings roughly 8m seasonally, and the authors suggest the species likely breeds in flooded riparian forest during high water.

  • Substrate & décor: No aquarium-specific decor guidance exists in the sources checked. The wild habitat data points strongly toward a thick leaf-litter layer and dense planting or submerged vegetation for cover — reproducing that directly, rather than relying on a generic apisto cave setup, is the best-supported choice available here.
  • Water chemistry: The original description's field measurements — pH 6.2–6.7, <1°dH, 5–20 µS/cm, 24–28°C — are genuinely soft and only mildly acidic, closer to this site's staecki than to the genus's extreme blackwater specialists. See our water parameters guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

The original description's aquarium observations describe sororcula as a polygynous cave spawner — a male holding a territory with multiple potential spawning sites, each one potentially the center of a smaller female territory nested within it. Beyond that breeding-specific structure, no source gives a general temperament or community-tank compatibility rating. Treat it like this site's other lesser-documented small apistos: calm tankmates, and no other Apistogramma species sharing the tank, to avoid both aggression and hybridization risk.

Breeding

Sororcula is a cave spawner with breeding behavior documented directly in its own original description — an unusual level of detail for a species this recently named. Staeck and Schindler recorded it as polygynous: a male defends a territory holding several potential spawning sites, and each site can become the center of a smaller territory held by one female within the male's larger one. Eggs are laid on the underside of a horizontal surface, with a stout plant leaf the preferred choice over a cave roof. At 27°C (81°F), hatching occurs roughly three days after spawning, and fry become free-swimming about five days after that. Brood care is maternal only — the female actively drives the male away from the spawning site itself, though he may continue defending the broader territory against predators. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process, adjusting toward the soft, mildly acidic water this species' field data supports.

Sourcing Notes

This is a fish with an unusually long hobby history under the wrong name. Amazonas Magazine's account of the 2016 description places its discovery in aquarium circles back in 1987, trading for 29 years under provisional names like "Apistogramma cf. staecki 'Rio Guaporé'" and "A. sp. Guaporé" before taxonomists gave it its own valid name. No source checked for this guide confirms whether the trade has widely shifted to calling it sororcula since, or documents a specific recent import under that name. Both FishBase and aquatab.net list its IUCN status as Not Evaluated, so apparent scarcity in retail listings may reflect naming confusion and thin documentation rather than any confirmed conservation concern. If you come across a fish sold as "cf. staecki," "sp. Guaporé," or similar, it's worth asking directly whether the seller means this now-valid species — see our general sourcing guide for broader guidance on verifying identification.

Final Thoughts

Sororcula is a genuine case of the hobby outrunning the science: aquarists kept and traded it for nearly three decades under placeholder names before Wolfgang Staeck — already honored by this site's own A. staecki three decades earlier — came back to formally describe it himself, fittingly naming it for its resemblance to his own namesake fish. If you want the established, better-documented relative first, start with staecki itself, or this site's far more available cacatuoides. If the genus's own tangled naming history is what draws you in, sororcula sits alongside trifasciata in the Rio Guaporé drainage and alongside erythrura and velifera on the list of species connected to Staeck's own fieldwork — a small fish with an outsized footprint in this site's own cross-referenced naming history.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the name Apistogramma sororcula mean, and how is it related to A. staecki?

FishBase's etymology note is explicit: the Latin epithet sororcula means "little sister," chosen by describers Wolfgang Staeck and Ingo Schindler specifically to reflect the species' similarity to and close relationship with this site's own A. staecki. The original 2016 description (Vertebrate Zoology 66(2): 141–150) backs that up with a list of concrete differences rather than just a family resemblance: sororcula's male caudal fin carries 10 to 12 (rarely 13) vertical bars against up to 8 in staecki, its lateral band runs all the way to the caudal spot where staecki's does not, all its dorsal-fin lappets are produced rather than just lappets 4 through 6, and males show a dark chin marking staecki's examined specimens lacked.

Why does this fish appear in hobby records decades before its 2016 scientific description?

Because aquarists had it in trade long before taxonomists caught up. Amazonas Magazine's coverage of the description places the fish's hobby discovery in 1987 — nearly three decades earlier — circulating under provisional trade names including "Apistogramma cf. staecki 'Rio Guaporé'" and "A. sp. Guaporé." The "cf." (Latin confer, "compare with") is a standard placeholder taxonomists and importers use for a fish that resembles a named species closely enough to provisionally group with it, pending formal description — exactly sororcula's situation for 29 years, and a strong practical reason to treat any current "cf. staecki" or "sp. Guaporé" listing as worth asking about by its now-valid name.

How big does Apistogramma sororcula get, and what water does it need?

FishBase gives a maximum size of 4.2cm (about 1.7in) standard length for males or unsexed fish; the holotype measured 40.1mm SL. The original description's own field measurements at the type locality — a río San Martín site in Bolivia's Beni department — recorded pH 6.2 to 6.7, total and temporary hardness under 1°dH, conductivity 5 to 20 µS/cm, and temperature 24 to 28°C (75–82°F): genuinely soft, only mildly acidic water, not the extreme blackwater chemistry this genus's harder specialists need. Is Apistogramma sororcula easy to breed?

The original description includes direct aquarium breeding observations, not just field data. The authors recorded it as a polygynous cave spawner: a male defends a territory containing several potential spawning sites, each of which can become the center of a smaller female's own territory within it. Eggs are laid on the underside of a horizontal surface — a stout plant leaf is the preferred choice — and at 27°C (81°F) hatch in about three days, with fry free-swimming roughly five days after that. Brood care is maternal; the female actively drives the male away from the spawning site itself, though he may still help defend the wider territory against predators.

Is Apistogramma sororcula available in the aquarium trade today?

No source checked for this guide documents a current retail listing under the valid name sororcula specifically. Its 29-year hobby history ran almost entirely under the "cf. staecki" and "sp. Guaporé" trade names described above, and no source found confirms whether exporters have widely adopted the 2016 name since. Both FishBase and aquatab.net list its IUCN status as Not Evaluated, and FishBase separately rates its fishing vulnerability low (10 of 100) — a species that appears under-threatened on paper but is thin on verified, recent, correctly-labeled trade documentation. Ask specifically for sororcula by name, and treat a "cf. staecki" tag as worth a closer look rather than a guaranteed match.
