Apistogramma luelingi

Apistogramma luelingi

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 3.3cm (1.3") SL male/unsexed, 2.9cm (1.1") SL female — FishBase; a conflicting trade source cites roughly double this, unconfirmed (see FAQ)
  • Tank: 24–40" (60–100cm); 75×30cm minimum footprint per experienced-keeper consensus
  • pH: 5.0–7.0 general; 6.4 in a documented successful spawn
  • Temperature: 72–79°F (22–26°C)
  • Hardness: GH 1–6 natural range; "very low" in a documented spawn
  • Temperament: Polygamous cave spawner; social, kept in pairs or small groups; male chases non-receptive females — normal, not incompatibility
  • Difficulty: Advanced — rare in trade, sensitive to abrupt parameter shifts, unresolved size and ID conflicts

Overview

Apistogramma luelingi is a small, rarely-traded dwarf cichlid from Bolivia and Peru, named for a German museum curator rather than a collector or hobbyist. Sven O. Kullander formally described it in 1976 (Bonner Zoologische Beiträge 27(3–4): 259, fig. 1), from a holotype (ZFMK 2283) and nine paratypes (ZFMK 2303 and ZFMK 2284–91). FishBase's etymology note credits the species name to Dr. Karl-Heinz Lüling (1913–1984), the first Curator and Chairman of the Ichthyology Department at Bonn's Forschungsmuseum Alexander Koenig, who retired from that post in 1978 — a dedication to a museum scientist rather than the aquarium trade.

Luelingi has a genuine place in this site's own running thread on Kullander's influence across the genus. This site's A. kullanderi page already documents how the 2014-described "titan among dwarfs" was named for Kullander, who had separately described this site's A. hongsloi in 1979 and, in a single 1986 monograph, both A. cruzi and A. juruensis. Luelingi pushes that same thread back a decade further than any of those: Kullander described it in 1976, three years before hongsloi and ten years before cruzi and juruensis — his earliest Apistogramma description represented on this site.

The original 1976 description itself contained an error its own author later fixed. Kullander gave the type locality as "R. Chapare, Todos Santos, Bolivia," but his own 1986 monograph (p. 177) corrected this to a more specific site: a ravine with an oxbow pool ("Altwasser") below San Francisco, beside the Río Chiripiri, Bolivia — a rare documented case of a describer revising his own fieldwork record a decade on.

Natural Habitat

FishBase lists luelingi's range as the Madre de Dios and Mamoré river basins of the Amazon basin, spanning Bolivia and Peru — a wider distribution than the single Bolivian ravine of the corrected type locality, reflecting records gathered in the decades since the original description. The species is benthopelagic in tropical freshwater, with FishBase citing a temperature range of 22–26°C (72–79°F). FishBase's own IUCN assessment, dated 25 April 2014, rates the species Least Concern — a healthier conservation picture than several of this site's other, more range-restricted species.

FishBase's morphological notes describe a moderately elongate body with 15 to 16 dorsal-fin spines and 5 to 6 soft rays, 3 to 4 anal-fin spines and 5 to 6 soft rays, and a longitudinal dark lateral band running the length of the body — genus-typical features. One specific note stands out: males up to 2.6cm SL show no fin modification yet, meaning the elongated fin extensions typical of adult male apistos in this genus develop only later in growth, after that size threshold.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: German retailer reference Aqualog recommends a minimum footprint of 60–100cm (24–40"). Independently, longtime apistogramma.com forum moderator Mike Wise gives his own practical minimum as 75×30cm, noting that tank height matters less than footprint since apistos rarely use water deeper than 25–30cm — a smaller tank can work if densely decorated, but the fish become harder to see. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: No source checked for this guide gives species-specific décor beyond genus-standard practice: caves or similar cover for spawning sites, and dense planting consistent with this species' soft, slightly acidic native water. French reference site Fishipedia specifically recommends peat filtration, decomposing leaf litter, or alder cones to help maintain acidic water.
  • Water chemistry: Sources converge on a pH range of roughly 5.0 to 7.0, narrower at 5 to 6.5 per Fishipedia, with natural GH cited at 1 to 6. A documented successful spawn occurred at pH 6.4 with very low hardness. Fishipedia specifically flags the species as sensitive to abrupt parameter swings and chemical exposure — don't treat this as a forgiving, beginner-tolerant apisto despite its small size. See our water parameters guide.
  • Water quality: Fishipedia recommends keeping nitrates under 25mg/L and a monthly water change of 20–30% of tank volume — notably gentler and less frequent than the weekly-change habits this site recommends for hardier apistos, consistent with the sensitivity noted above. See our water quality guide.

Feeding

Aqualog describes luelingi as accepting dry flakes, frozen food, and live food, while Fishipedia characterizes it as omnivorous with a carnivorous lean, recommending a varied diet of dry, fresh, and frozen foods fed in small portions the fish can finish in two to three minutes, to avoid fouling the water in a tank already sensitive to parameter swings. See our general feeding guide for broader conditioning advice ahead of breeding.

Temperament & Compatibility

Aqualog describes luelingi as a social, diurnal fish best kept in pairs. Fishipedia adds more nuance: it can be aggressive, particularly toward its own kind, and shouldn't be mixed with large, territorial, or otherwise aggressive tankmates, though it can cohabit with peaceful species; large fish and crustaceans should be avoided as potential predators rather than rivals. On the breeding side specifically, Mike Wise's forum account describes male luelingi as persistently chasing every female in the tank except whichever one is currently in breeding condition — normal polygamous behavior for this genus, not a sign of incompatibility, but worth planning tank layout around (see Breeding, below).

Breeding

Luelingi is a polygamous cave spawner, per FishBase: eggs are attached to the ceiling of a cave, and the female alone guards both the eggs and the resulting larvae — no paternal involvement in brood care. On the apistogramma.com forum, moderator Mike Wise lays out a specific, practical breeding setup born from exactly the male-chasing behavior noted above: remove the male from the tank, rearrange the décor to create two clearly distinct potential breeding territories, introduce two females, let them settle into their own chosen territories, then reintroduce the male — giving him two established, separately-defended spawning sites rather than one contested one. A separate forum account from hobbyist "garcia" documents a successful spawn achieved in a 20-gallon tank at pH 6.4 with very low hardness, a useful real-world data point close to the water chemistry documented above. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process, adjusted toward this soft, mildly acidic chemistry.

Sourcing Notes

True luelingi is a genuinely uncommon trade fish, not a store staple. Fishipedia describes it as rare in the aquarium trade, maintained mostly by experienced hobbyists breeding lines descended from wild-caught stock, and recommends working through specialist aquarium clubs rather than general retail to track it down. A separate, documented wrinkle complicates sourcing further: "Apistogramma cf. luelingi 'Cristal,'" a trade variant Mike Wise obtained as a breeding pair collected near Puerto Maldonado, Peru, by a Peruvian collector in 2012. Its females develop short tips at the top and bottom of the caudal fin, a feature Wise says isn't present in typical luelingi — and he states plainly that he considers it "probably a separate species," though no source checked for this guide confirms that formally. Treat any fish sold as "cf. luelingi" or "Cristal" as provisionally identified rather than confirmed true luelingi, and ask a seller directly about collection locality and any fin differences before buying. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting these kinds of claims.

Final Thoughts

Luelingi is a quiet entry in this site's library in most respects — a small, soft-water Bolivian-Peruvian apisto without the dramatic size or conservation stakes of some neighbors — but it carries two genuinely open questions a careful keeper should know about going in: a scientific-versus-trade size conflict no source resolves, and an unconfirmed possible second species hiding under its own "cf." trade name. It also deepens this site's running Kullander thread a decade further back than any other entry, connecting to kullanderi, hongsloi, cruzi, and juruensis. If you want a better-documented, more available Bolivian-range relative first, borellii is far more common in the trade, though it comes from a separate river system entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who was Apistogramma luelingi named after, and is it connected to other species on this site?

FishBase's etymology note credits the name to Dr. Karl-Heinz Lüling (1913–1984), a German ichthyologist who served as the first Curator and Chairman of the Ichthyology Department at the Forschungsmuseum Alexander Koenig in Bonn, retiring in 1978. The describer, Sven O. Kullander, formally named luelingi in 1976 (Bonner Zoologische Beiträge 27(3–4): 259) — a decade before the 1986 monograph that this site's own A. cruzi and A. juruensis pages trace to the same taxonomist, and seven years after Kullander's first Apistogramma description, this site's own A. hongsloi, in 1979. Luelingi extends that same Kullander throughline this site already documents on its A. kullanderi page — a species named in Kullander's own honor — back an extra decade.

How big does Apistogramma luelingi get? Sources disagree sharply.

They do, and the gap is too large to paper over. FishBase gives a maximum of 3.3cm (1.3in) standard length for males or unsexed fish and 2.9cm (1.1in) for females — a genuinely small apisto. German retailer reference Aqualog, by contrast, lists males at roughly 7cm and females at roughly 4cm, more than double FishBase's figure for males. No source checked for this guide resolves the discrepancy; it may reflect a standard-length-versus-total-length mismatch, a confusion with the larger "Cristal" trade variant discussed below, or simply an error in one source. Treat FishBase's scientifically-documented figure as the more conservative baseline and the Aqualog figure as unconfirmed.

What water chemistry and tank size does Apistogramma luelingi need?

FishBase lists its natural habitat as tropical freshwater at 22–26°C (72–79°F); hobbyist reference sources narrow the pH to a 5.0–7.0 range generally, with French reference site Fishipedia giving natural GH as 1–6 and a documented successful spawn (reported by hobbyist "garcia" on the apistogramma.com forum) occurring at pH 6.4 with very low hardness in a 20-gallon tank. German retailer Aqualog recommends a minimum footprint of 60–100cm; longtime forum moderator Mike Wise independently gives 75×30cm as his own practical minimum, noting height matters less since apistos rarely use water deeper than 25–30cm. See our general water parameters guide and tank setup guide for the broader context.

Is Apistogramma luelingi easy to breed?

FishBase documents it as a polygamous cave spawner: eggs are attached to the ceiling of a cave, and the female alone guards both eggs and larvae. On the apistogramma.com forum, moderator Mike Wise describes a male's pattern of chasing every female except whichever one is currently in breeding condition as normal polygamous behavior, not a compatibility problem, and recommends a deliberate setup — remove the male, rearrange decor into two distinct territories, add two females, let them settle, then reintroduce the male — specifically to protect a single receptive female from constant harassment. One forum-documented spawn succeeded in a 20-gallon tank at pH 6.4 with very low hardness, roughly matching the water chemistry above. See our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process.

Is Apistogramma luelingi available in the aquarium trade, and what is the "Cristal" variant?

French reference site Fishipedia describes true luelingi as genuinely rare in the trade, kept mostly by experienced hobbyists maintaining captive lines descended from wild-caught stock, and recommends going through specialist aquarium clubs to source it rather than general retail. A separate trade form, "Apistogramma cf. luelingi 'Cristal,'" adds a documented identification wrinkle: Mike Wise reports that a Peruvian collector gathered his breeding pair near Puerto Maldonado, Peru, in 2012, and that its females develop short tips at the top and bottom of the caudal fin — a feature not seen in typical luelingi. Wise calls it "probably a separate species," though the forum thread stops short of confirming that, leaving the "cf." (Latin confer, "compare with") designation as an open, unresolved question still circulating in the hobby today.
