Apistogramma steindachneri

Steindachner's Dwarf Cichlid

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Up to about 3.5" (9 cm) — one of the larger commonly-kept apistos
  • Tank: 30+ gallons, at least 40" (100 cm) long
  • pH: 5.5–7.0
  • Temperature: 73–82°F (23–28°C)
  • Hardness: Soft, blackwater conditions preferred
  • Temperament: Variable — reported as anything from mellow to genuinely pushy
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Apistogramma steindachneri is one of the largest commonly-kept apistos, reaching 3.5 inches with a heavy, blunt head, and needs meaningfully more tank space than beginner species like cacatuoides or borellii. It is a bigger, blunter-headed cousin to the classic apistos on this site — a stretched, compressed body topped with a heavy, massive head unlike the more delicate look of cacatuoides or borellii. Base color runs yellowish to light brown, lighter underneath, with a black lateral band, a black spot at the base of the tail, and a dark bar running through the eye. Gill-cover patterning varies noticeably by locality.

In the wild it's found in drainage canals and similar habitats with loamy, leaf-covered substrate and acidic water — a genuine blackwater specialist, closer in that respect to nijsseni than to the more flexible macmasteri.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: Give this one real room — a 100 cm (about 40") tank or larger is the usual recommendation, well above what cacatuoides or borellii need.
  • Substrate: Fine gravel or sand.
  • Hardscape: Roots, caves, and rock structures for a well-broken-up layout. A true blackwater setup with leaf litter and driftwood tannins is especially well suited to this species.
  • Water chemistry: Acidic, soft water is where this species does best — don't expect the hardware tolerance of a macmasteri.

Feeding

Standard apisto omnivore diet: quality flake and pellet as a base, supplemented with live or frozen bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia. Given its larger size, a steindachneri can also handle slightly larger food items than the smaller species on this site.

Temperament & Compatibility

This is the species where you'll see the widest range of anecdotal reports — some keepers describe a calm, easygoing fish; others report real pushiness, especially from larger males. Plan for the more assertive end: give it more space than a cacatuoides, avoid crowding, and watch a new male's behavior closely before assuming it's harmless. Its larger size also means it can be a legitimate threat to genuinely small tankmates.

Breeding

Breeding follows the standard apisto pattern — cave spawning, female-led egg and fry guarding, male perimeter defense — but expect the process to demand more consistent soft, acidic water than the more adaptable species in this genus. Given the size and temperament variability, breeding pairs benefit from a larger, more heavily structured tank than you'd set up for cacatuoides, so territorial disputes have somewhere to dissipate.

A Note on Identification

Steindachneri sits in a cluster of closely related, frequently-confused species alongside macmasteri and viejita — even experienced keepers sometimes disagree on IDs from photos alone, particularly for females. If a confident, locality-backed ID matters to you, buy from a specialist rather than a general retailer.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Less commonly available than cacatuoides or borellii. Specialist apisto breeders and importers are the more reliable sources for a confidently-identified fish. See our sourcing guide for what to ask before you buy.

Final Thoughts

Steindachneri is a good pick for a keeper who wants scale — a bigger, bolder-looking apisto — and has the tank space and blackwater setup to match. It's not a species to drop into a small, lightly-planted community tank; give it room and genuinely soft water, and it rewards you with a striking, substantial fish.
