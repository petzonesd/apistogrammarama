Apistogramma bitaeniata

Two-Striped Dwarf Cichlid

By Pet Zone SD Team • October 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Male to 3" (7.5 cm), female to 2" (5 cm)
  • Tank: 20 gallons minimum, 24"+ long for a pair or harem
  • pH: 5.0–6.5 — leans toward blackwater
  • Temperature: 77–84°F (25–29°C)
  • Hardness: Soft, 1–8 dGH
  • Temperament: Semi-aggressive; males territorial with each other
  • Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced

Overview

Apistogramma bitaeniata, the two-striped dwarf cichlid, is a warmer-water blackwater specialist from the upper Amazon that trades the extreme water chemistry of baenschi or elizabethae for a bigger adult size closer to agassizii and macmasteri. It's found across tributaries of the upper Amazon in Peru, Brazil, Ecuador, and Colombia, where it shelters among leaf litter and submerged wood in slow, tannin-stained water. The common name comes from two dark horizontal stripes running the length of the body — one high, one low — that stay visible on both sexes regardless of how much color the male develops.

Mature males carry a warm yellow-orange body with an extended, rounded caudal fin marked by a fine red net pattern, which is why some of the hobby's nicer strains get sold as "redtail." Females are smaller, plainer outside of brooding, and flash yellow when guarding fry — the same family pattern seen across the genus. Because several close relatives and regional forms travel under the same trade name, it's common to see stock labeled "Apistogramma cf. bitaeniata," meaning the seller isn't claiming certainty on the exact identification.

Tank Setup

Give a pair or small harem at least 20 gallons with 24 inches of length — bitaeniata runs closer to agassizii's size than to a nano species like borellii, and males want room to establish separate territories.

  • Substrate: Fine, dark sand. This species is a committed leaf-litter and substrate forager.
  • Leaf litter: Strongly recommended, not optional. Indian almond or oak leaves recreate the blackwater margins this species comes from and help hold pH down.
  • Hardscape: Driftwood and dense planting with multiple sightline breaks and caves — one per female in a harem setup.
  • Filtration: Gentle flow. This is slow-water habitat, not a current-loving species.
  • Lighting: Dim to moderate, with floating plants to cut glare and bring out the male's color.
  • Heating: Run toward the warm end, 79–84°F, rather than the middle of the genus's usual range.

Feeding

An omnivore that forages leaf litter and substrate for insect larvae and crustaceans in the wild. In the tank, a quality micro-pellet or flake base supplemented with live or frozen bloodworms, baby brine shrimp, and daphnia keeps color and condition up. Feed twice daily in amounts the fish clear in a couple of minutes.

Temperament & Compatibility

More assertive than agassizii or trifasciata — males spar over territory and a crowded tank can turn tense fast. Keep one male to two or three females rather than a single pair, with enough visual breaks that each female can hold her own space. Avoid housing with other Apistogramma species in anything short of a very large, heavily broken-up tank, since hybridization and aggression both become risks once territories overlap.

Peaceful community fish that stay out of the bottom territory — tetras, hatchetfish, and corydoras — make good tankmates. As with the rest of the genus, dwarf shrimp are not safe long-term once the fish reach adult size.

Breeding

Conditioning

Condition the group on live and frozen foods for two to three weeks. Breeding success tracks water chemistry closely here — soft, acidic, warm water gets far more reliable spawns than water that merely meets the minimum on paper.

Spawning

A cave spawner. The female claims a cave or similarly enclosed space, lays her eggs on the ceiling or wall, and takes over almost all brood defense, while the male patrols and defends the wider territory from rivals.

Fry Care

Eggs hatch in roughly 2–3 days, with fry free-swimming about a week after that. Start fry on infusoria or a liquid fry food before stepping up to newly hatched brine shrimp and microworms. Hold water quality steady through this stage — ammonia and nitrite swings hit fry hard regardless of how tolerant the adults are of day-to-day drift.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Because the bitaeniata complex includes several close relatives and regional forms, ask the seller whether the fish is confirmed bitaeniata or sold as "cf." — and if locality matters to your breeding goals, ask where the line traces back to. Pick individuals that forage actively and show clean, undamaged fins; a bitaeniata that hangs at the back of the tank with washed-out color is usually stressed or poorly acclimated.

Final Thoughts

If macmasteri or agassizii felt manageable and you want to push toward true blackwater conditions without jumping straight to baenschi or elizabethae, bitaeniata is the logical next step — bigger and feistier than the beginner species, but not yet demanding the razor-thin water chemistry of the genus's most advanced specialists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Apistogramma bitaeniata called the two-striped dwarf cichlid?

Both sexes show two dark horizontal stripes running the length of the body — one along the upper flank, one lower — which stay visible even as the male's yellow-orange base color and red finnage develop with age.

Is Apistogramma bitaeniata a beginner fish?

No. It's better suited to a keeper who has already bred an easier species like cacatuoides, since it wants warmer, softer, more acidic water than most apistos and the males can be feistier toward each other.

How big does bitaeniata get?

Males reach about 3 inches and females around 2 inches, putting it in the same larger size bracket as agassizii and macmasteri rather than with the smaller nano species like borellii.

What water parameters does bitaeniata need?

Soft and acidic — pH 5.0 to 6.5, hardness under 8 dGH — and on the warmer end for the genus at 77 to 84°F. It leans closer to a blackwater specialist than a generalist like agassizii.

Why do I see "cf. bitaeniata" on some fish for sale?

"cf." (confer) signals the seller isn't fully certain the fish matches the species description, which is common in this group since several close relatives and regional forms circulate under the bitaeniata name. Buy from a seller who can speak to the specific locality or line if that matters to you.
