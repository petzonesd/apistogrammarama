Apistogramma paucisquamis

Apistogramma paucisquamis

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: ~1" (2.5–3.4cm SL) — small even for the genus
  • Tank: Not documented species-specifically; genus-standard 15–20g given its small size
  • pH: 4.5–6.0 (wild type-locality reading: 4.3)
  • Temperature: 77–82°F (type locality measured 79°F/26°C)
  • Hardness: Under 5 dGH; under 2 dGH ideal for breeding (wild reading: <1°dH)
  • Temperament: Males display an open-mouthed frontal threat — a trait shared only with this site's cacatuoides and agassizii groups
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genus-extreme blackwater chemistry and genuine trade rarity

Overview

Apistogramma paucisquamis is named for a trait, not a place or a person: it carries only 12 circumpeduncular scale rows around the tail, where almost every other Apistogramma has 16. Sven O. Kullander and Wolfgang Staeck formally described the species in 1988 in the French journal Cybium, choosing a name built straight from Latin — paucus ("few") plus squama ("scale") — for that single diagnostic feature. The type series, 96 specimens between 12.8 and 31.2mm, came from a shallow bay on the right bank of Brazil's Rio Negro in the Anavilhanas archipelago, collected by Staeck himself on March 27, 1986; the holotype, an adult male, is held at MZUSP (catalog 36952), with paratypes split across MZUSP, NRM, USNM, and ZFMK.

The fish wasn't unknown before that. German aquarists had already been keeping it informally as the "Glanzbinden-Apistogramma" ("shine-band apisto"), and in a genuine pre-description mix-up, Hermann Meinken folded one paucisquamis specimen into the paratype series of this site's own A. gibbiceps back in 1969 — nineteen years before Kullander and Staeck sorted the two apart for good.

Appearance & Identification

Beyond the scale count, males show a lyre-shaped tail with streamer extensions off the third dorsal and third ventral fin rays, thickened lips, somewhat elongated jaws, and blue markings along the flanks. One behavioral trait is a genuine rarity in the genus: males perform an open-mouthed frontal threat display toward rivals, a pattern documented in the literature as shared only with the agassizii and cacatuoides species groups — both of which this site already covers in A. agassizii and A. cacatuoides.

Natural Habitat

Paucisquamis is a Rio Negro blackwater endemic, documented along the stretch running from the Anavilhanas archipelago down toward Santa Isabel do Rio Negro and Manaus. At the type locality, Staeck's own in-situ water reading was extreme even by the standards of this genus: 79°F (26°C), pH 4.3, and hardness under 1°dH — true Amazonian blackwater with almost no mineral content at all.

The species carries a real, sourced conservation flag rather than a guessed one: the IUCN assessed it as Data Deficient in an evaluation dated November 7, 2018 and published in 2022, explicitly describing the species as "infrequent and not abundant" in the wild and naming ornamental-trade collection as a specific pressure. It is not CITES-listed.

Tank Setup

No source checked for this guide documents a tested aquarium setup with the species-specific detail available for better-known fish on this site — paucisquamis is simply too rare in the hobby for that yet. What follows combines the wild-water readings above with genus-standard defaults for a fish this small.

  • Tank size: Not documented species-specifically. Given an adult size around 1 inch, a 15–20 gallon tank is a reasonable genus-standard starting point. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: Not documented species-specifically, but a Rio Negro blackwater origin points toward a soft, dark substrate with driftwood and a real leaf litter layer over an open layout.
  • Water chemistry: Target pH 4.5–6.0 and GH under 5 dGH day-to-day, tightening toward 2 dGH or lower if attempting to breed — see our water parameters guide for how to get there from harder tap water.
  • Filtration & flow: Not documented species-specifically. A gentle-flow sponge filter is the genus-standard default for a small blackwater specialist like this one.

Follow genus-standard practice: a quality small-pellet or flake food as a daily base, with live or frozen foods like baby brine shrimp, daphnia, and bloodworm for conditioning and color. Temperament & Compatibility

Documentation here is thin, but the open-mouthed frontal threat display between rival males is a genuine, specific behavioral note rather than a generic difficulty rating — and it's a trait this species shares with only two other species groups in the genus. No broader compatibility data (tankmates, group dynamics beyond male rivalry) was found in sources checked for this guide. As with every species on this site, don't house multiple Apistogramma species together given the real hybridization risk — a risk this particular species makes concrete, since it's documented sharing actual wild habitat with another species this site covers (see Sourcing Notes below).

Breeding

Paucisquamis is a standard cave-spawning harem polygamist, but a genuinely difficult one to breed consistently. Breeders discussing the species on the apistogramma.com forum report that success hinges on holding pH between 4.5 and 5.5, conductivity under 50 µS/cm, and conditioning a pair for months before a reliable spawn — sustained genus-extreme water chemistry, not a quick setup job. No source checked for this guide documents egg counts, incubation time, or fry-rearing specifics for this species; follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process until more detail is published, and expect the water-chemistry demands above to matter more here than the general steps do.

Sourcing Notes

Paucisquamis is a genuine trade rarity. The one documented account of its supply chain comes from German wholesale importer Aquarium Glaser, which has reported receiving the species mixed into shipments of this site's own A. mendezi collected east of Barcelos on the Rio Negro — writing that the importer believes the two "live together in the wild." That's a real, sourced case of two species on this site sharing actual wild habitat, not just a generic range overlap. Because the two look alike enough to confuse when calm or frightened, ask a seller to confirm which species a "mendezi" or "paucisquamis" shipment actually is, or check the caudal fin yourself: vertically striped points to paucisquamis, broadly striped or spotted points to mendezi. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's identification.

Final Thoughts

Paucisquamis is a specialist's fish built around two real hooks: a genuinely unusual anatomical trait that gave the species its name decades after German hobbyists already knew the fish informally, and a documented case of sharing actual Rio Negro habitat with this site's own mendezi — close enough that even wholesale importers mix the two up. If you already keep mendezi and want the species it's actually been shipped alongside in the wild, this is the natural next challenge. If you want a far more available, far better-documented entry point into the genus first, this site's own cacatuoides remains the better starting place.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Apistogramma paucisquamis unusual among dwarf cichlids?

It carries only 12 circumpeduncular scale rows around the tail, compared to 16 in virtually every other Apistogramma — the trait its 1988 describers, Sven Kullander and Wolfgang Staeck, named it for (Latin paucus, "few," plus squama, "scale"). German aquarists knew the fish informally as the "Glanzbinden-Apistogramma" before its formal description, and one specimen was even mistakenly folded into the paratype series of this site's own A. gibbiceps by Hermann Meinken in 1969, nineteen years before paucisquamis got its own name.

What water parameters does Apistogramma paucisquamis need?

Extreme blackwater. At the type locality in Brazil's Rio Negro (the Anavilhanas archipelago), co-describer Wolfgang Staeck measured 79°F (26°C), pH 4.3, and hardness under 1°dH in situ. In the aquarium, aim for pH 4.5–6.0 and GH under 5 dGH, dropping toward 2 dGH or lower for breeding attempts — genus-extreme blackwater territory similar to this site's own mendezi.

Does Apistogramma paucisquamis share habitat with any other species covered on this site?

Yes, and it's the species' most interesting documented trait. German wholesale importer Aquarium Glaser has reported receiving paucisquamis mixed into shipments of this site's own A. mendezi from east of Barcelos on the Rio Negro, writing that the two "we think live together in the wild." The two look similar enough when calm or frightened that the clearest way to separate males is the caudal fin pattern: vertically striped in paucisquamis, broadly striped or spotted in mendezi.

Is Apistogramma paucisquamis easy to breed?

No. It's a standard cave-spawning harem polygamist, but breeders on the apistogramma.com forum report success depends on pH 4.5–5.5, conductivity under 50 µS/cm, and months of conditioning before a pair spawns reliably — sustained genus-extreme water chemistry rather than a quick setup. The IUCN lists the species as Data Deficient (assessed 2018, published 2022), explicitly describing it as "infrequent and not abundant" in the wild, with ornamental-trade collection flagged as a named pressure.
