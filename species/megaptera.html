Apistogramma megaptera

Apistogramma megaptera

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Disputed — Aquarium Glaser gives ~7cm (2.75") males/~4cm (1.6") females; Fishipedia gives a 5cm average/6cm max with no sex split
  • Tank: Documented successful breeding in a planted 10-gallon; Fishipedia separately recommends 100L (26g) minimum for general keeping
  • pH: 4.5–6.5 per modern sources; a documented 1998 breeding account ran "in the 6's"
  • Temperature: 24–28°C (75–82°F) per modern sources; documented breeding account ran ~78°F (25.6°C)
  • Hardness: Not documented by any source checked for this guide
  • Temperament: Fishipedia calls it "slightly aggressive" and territorial toward conspecifics; Aquarium Glaser simply calls it "hardy"
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genuinely rare in the trade, and modern sources call breeding method "unknown" despite a documented 1998 success

Overview

Apistogramma megaptera spent decades in the hobby as a nameless trade fish before it had a scientific name to go with its description. Mesa S. & Lasso formally described the species in 2011, but German aquarium exporter Aquarium Glaser — whose own fish lexicon explains the scientific name as Greek for "with very large fins" — had already been selling it as "Apistogramma sp. Breitbinden" for years before that, and hobbyists further back still: a 1998 Greater Seattle Aquarium Society (GSAS) article by breeder Kathy Olson documents keeping it under that same trade name, sourced through the Internet Apisto List, more than a decade before the formal description existed. "Breitbinden" is German for "broad band," referring to the broad dark band running under the eye — and both names are still used side by side in the trade today.

This site's own A. brevis page separately notes an undescribed "Apistogramma sp. 'breitbinden'" sharing the Rio Uaupés with brevis — a different river system from this species' own Upper Orinoco range (see Natural Habitat below). Whether that's the same taxon carrying the same descriptive trade name, or an entirely separate population that happens to share it, isn't resolved by any source checked for this guide. Treat the two as a genuinely open question rather than the same fish.

Appearance & Identification

Sources genuinely disagree on adult size. Aquarium Glaser gives males at roughly 7cm (2.75") and females at roughly 4cm (1.6") — a substantial sexual size difference. Fishipedia instead gives a single average of 5cm with a 6cm maximum and no separate male/female figures. Neither source explains the gap, and this guide isn't resolving it. The "megaptera" name itself — "with very large fins" per Aquarium Glaser's lexicon — points to large fins as the species' most visually distinctive trait, consistent with the broad dark eye-band that earned it the "Breitbinden" trade name long before it had a Latin one.

Natural Habitat

Both sources checked for this guide place the species in the Upper Orinoco basin of Colombia and Venezuela. Aquarium Glaser is the more specific of the two, naming the Rio Inírida, Rio Atabapo, and Rio Mavaca; Fishipedia names the Atabapo and Inírida only. Both describe a blackwater biotope stained by dissolved humus — consistent with the soft, acidic water both sources recommend for keeping it (see Tank Setup below).

No source checked for this guide gives a confirmed numeric hardness reading for either the wild habitat or an aquarium. Aquarium Glaser says only that water "should be soft and acidic," at least for breeding, without a number attached.

Tank Setup

Documented husbandry for this species leans on a handful of sources rather than a broad base of care writing, and they don't fully agree with each other.

  • Tank size: Fishipedia recommends a minimum of 100 liters (about 26 gallons) for general keeping. The one documented breeding account checked for this guide — Kathy Olson's 1998 GSAS report — used a 10-gallon tank for spawning, considerably smaller; treat the two figures as describing different purposes (general keeping vs. a dedicated spawning tank) rather than a contradiction. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: Aquarium Glaser specifies fine sand. Olson's breeding tank was "heavily planted" with Java fern, Bolbitis heudelotii, floating frogbit, and water sprite; her account doesn't mention a cave, pot, or other dedicated spawning site, unusual for a genus where most documented breeding accounts on this site center on one.
  • Water chemistry: Fishipedia gives pH 4.5–6.5 and 24–28°C (75–82°F); Aquarium Glaser agrees broadly that water should be soft and acidic, especially for breeding, at 26–28°C. Olson's documented breeding tank ran pH "in the 6's" at around 78°F (25.6°C) — within both ranges, though less extreme than Fishipedia's lower pH bound. See our water parameters guide for how to reach this range from typical tap water.
  • Water quality: Fishipedia specifies keeping nitrate under 50mg/L with 20–30% monthly water changes and recommends peat filtration plus decaying leaves or alder cones to maintain acidity — genus-standard blackwater practice. See our water quality guide.

Feeding

Neither primary source checked for this guide documents a detailed adult feeding regimen beyond general genus practice. Olson's 1998 account is more specific about fry: newly hatched fish were started on baby brine shrimp, then moved onto flake food, adult brine shrimp, Daphnia, and mosquito larvae as juveniles (see Breeding below). For the general genus-standard approach to adult feeding, see our feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

Modern and historical sources describe this species differently. Fishipedia rates its behavior "slightly aggressive," calling it territorial and capable of being "virulent" toward its own kind, and recommends keeping it in a group with plenty of hiding places outside of breeding. Aquarium Glaser, by contrast, simply calls the species "hardy" without flagging aggression. Olson's 1998 account sits closer to Fishipedia's description: the male she kept displayed aggressively at other fish in his tank, and when a juvenile female was introduced she initially hid, then turned the tables — displaying back at him and chasing him off — before the pair spawned roughly two weeks later. As with every species on this site, avoid mixed-species Apistogramma tanks given the documented cross-spawning risk covered on other species pages (see this site's own tucurui page for a specific example).

Breeding

Modern and historical sources flatly disagree on whether this species' breeding behavior is even documented at all. Fishipedia states the breeding method is "still unknown," rates breeding difficulty "hard," recommends starting with at least five fish to let pairs form naturally, and warns that fry survival in a community tank is "almost null." But a detailed 1998 Greater Seattle Aquarium Society account by Kathy Olson (photos by Erik Olson) documents a real, successful spawn that directly contradicts the "unknown" claim. A trio sourced through the Internet Apisto List — identified as "Breitbinden" by Uwe Römer from photographs, and carried from Chicago to Seattle by hobbyist David Soares — produced a pair that spawned in a heavily planted 10-gallon tank within about two weeks of the female's introduction. The male was removed from the tank immediately after spawning. Roughly 30 fry were raised: started on baby brine shrimp, then graduated to flake food, adult brine shrimp, Daphnia, and mosquito larvae as juveniles. Growth was documented as slow — the fish were still not full adult size seven months later. Neither Olson's account nor either modern source documents who guarded the fry, egg count, or fry-rearing water-change schedule, so treat this as a real but incomplete breeding record rather than a full protocol. See our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process this genus follows.

Sourcing Notes

This is a genuinely rare aquarium fish, not a common one with thin documentation. Fishipedia lists its hobby availability as "rare," calling it "very rare in the aquarium trade" and recommending contact with specialized clubs or societies to find stock. Aquarium Glaser's own archived listing is from 2012 and describes a wholesale-only wild-collected shipment — dated information that shouldn't be assumed to reflect current availability. The 1998 GSAS account likewise describes sourcing fish through a hobbyist trading network (the Internet Apisto List) rather than a retail purchase. If you're looking for this species specifically, expect to go through a specialty breeder, a dedicated cichlid club, or a wild-import specialist rather than a general aquarium store — and confirm current stock with any seller before assuming availability. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller.

Final Thoughts

Megaptera is a genuine hobby rarity with an unusually documented history for how little-known it remains today: traded under the "Breitbinden" name for over a decade before it had a scientific name, bred successfully by at least one hobbyist in 1998, and yet still described by a modern vendor reference as having an "unknown" breeding method. The size and temperament disagreements between sources are real and unresolved — this guide presents both rather than picking a winner. If you're drawn to the broad-eye-band "Breitbinden" look but want an easier path into blackwater Apistogramma keeping first, this site's own A. brevis page covers a related-by-trade-name (but not confirmed related-by-taxonomy) species from a different river system entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Apistogramma megaptera mean, and why is it also called "Breitbinden"?

Megaptera is Greek for "with very large fins." Mesa S. & Lasso formally described the species in 2011, but it had already circulated in the hobby for decades under the German trade name "Apistogramma sp. Breitbinden" — "broad band" — after the broad dark band running under the eye. Both names are still used side by side today.

How big does Apistogramma megaptera get?

Sources genuinely disagree. German wholesale importer Aquarium Glaser gives males around 7cm (2.75") and females around 4cm (1.6"). The hobbyist reference Fishipedia instead gives an average of 5cm with a 6cm maximum, with no separate male/female figures. This guide notes both rather than picking one.

Is Apistogramma megaptera easy to breed?

Modern sources say no — Fishipedia rates breeding difficulty "hard" and describes the breeding method itself as still unknown, warning that fry survival in a community tank is "almost null." But a detailed 1998 Greater Seattle Aquarium Society (GSAS) account by breeder Kathy Olson documents a real, successful spawn: a pair bred within about two weeks of introduction in a planted 10-gallon tank, and roughly 30 fry were raised to several months old. This guide flags the contradiction rather than resolving it.

Where does Apistogramma megaptera come from, and what water does it need?

The Upper Orinoco basin in Colombia and Venezuela — German wholesaler Aquarium Glaser names the Rio Inírida, Rio Atabapo, and Rio Mavaca specifically, a blackwater habitat stained by dissolved humus. Modern sources recommend soft, acidic water (Fishipedia: pH 4.5–6.5, 24–28°C); the 1998 GSAS breeding account used pH "in the 6's" at about 78°F (25.6°C), with no hardness reading recorded by either source.
