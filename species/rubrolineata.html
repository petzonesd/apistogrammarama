Apistogramma rubrolineata

Apistogramma rubrolineata

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 4.0cm (1.6") SL max per original description — one of the smaller Apistogramma species
  • Tank: No species-specific minimum documented; standard apisto footprint, shallow natural habitat (~2ft)
  • pH: 4.5–6.5 (sources genuinely disagree — see FAQ); 6.0 in a documented home-tank breeding
  • Temperature: 75–80°F (24–27°C) across documented wild and tank reports
  • Hardness: 17–280 ppm TDS (wide documented spread between a Peruvian wild site and a Bolivian-side breeder)
  • Temperament: Cave spawner; reportedly hardy and less aggressive than many Apistogramma
  • Difficulty: Advanced (genuine trade rarity + a documented, unresolved sexing problem)

Overview

Apistogramma rubrolineata is a genuine rarity of the Apistogramma trade, described from a single Bolivian river system in 2002 and still only irregularly available through specialist importers today. Gunther Hein, Alfred Zarske, and Jaime Zapata formally described it in the December 2002 issue of Das Aquarium, the German vivaristics magazine (volume 36, issue 402, pages 15–19), from material collected at the Río Manuripi in Departamento Pando, Bolivia — a blackwater river with loamy banks and a type-locality pH around 5.9. The Catalog of Fishes maintained by the California Academy of Sciences lists the original description under reference number 26552. The species was reportedly first collected in 2001, with formal description following the next year.

The name itself is descriptive rather than honorific: Latin "rubrolineata" means "red-lined," referring to seven brown-red longitudinal stripes running along the body — a pattern German wholesale exporter Aquarium Glaser notes is only fully expressed in certain moods of dominant males, rather than visible at all times in every fish. FishBase lists a maximum size of 4.0cm standard length, making it one of the smaller species in a genus already known for small fish, and rates its IUCN status as Data Deficient — formally assessed but without enough data for a conservation category, reflecting how little has been published about it since its description.

Natural Habitat

The formal and field records for this species' range genuinely don't agree, and that discrepancy is worth presenting honestly rather than smoothing over. FishBase and the original description both treat rubrolineata as known only from its Bolivian type locality, the Río Manuripi. But a detailed October 2017 wild-collection and breeding report published by the Potomac Valley Aquarium Society (PVAS) documents a second population from a creek the report calls "Alegria," in the Madre de Dios drainage of Peru — hundreds of kilometers from the Río Manuripi. That Peruvian site was measured at 17ppm TDS, pH 6.5, and 75°F (24°C), in shallow water roughly two feet deep flowing clear over mud and gravel. No source checked for this guide formally reconciles the Bolivia-only type locality against the Peruvian field report; the most defensible reading is that the taxonomically confirmed range is the Río Manuripi, Bolivia, while the Peruvian population is a credible but not yet formally described extension.

That Peruvian locality sits in the same broad Madre de Dios border region this site's own A. luelingi page already covers — luelingi's documented range spans the Madre de Dios and Mamoré basins across both Bolivia and Peru, and one of its own trade variants was collected near Puerto Maldonado, itself in Peru's Madre de Dios department. The two species aren't known to be close relatives, but a keeper assembling a Bolivia/Madre-de-Dios-region biotope tank has a genuine reason to research both together.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: No source checked for this guide documents a species-specific minimum. The wild habitat is shallow — roughly 2 feet of water — so a standard low-to-moderate-depth apisto setup rather than a tall tank is a reasonable starting point. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: The documented breeding account used a clay pot as a spawning cave, with the female entering head-first to guard it. The wild site runs over mud and gravel in clear, flowing water — dense planting, driftwood, and leaf litter suit the species' generally soft, acidic native conditions without needing to replicate blackwater staining specifically.
  • Water chemistry: Documented reports genuinely disagree — the Peruvian wild site measured pH 6.5 at 17ppm TDS, the same keeper's home tank ran pH 6 at 60ppm TDS, and a separate Bolivian-side breeder reported pH 4.5–5.5 at roughly 280ppm TDS. Until more reports narrow this down, plan for a pH 4.5–6.5 range rather than targeting one figure precisely. See our water parameters guide.
  • Water quality: No species-specific maintenance schedule is documented beyond the general genus standard of regular partial water changes. See our water quality guide.

Feeding

The documented PVAS breeding account describes a straightforward, unfussy diet: flake food (with brands rotated) in the morning, and live or frozen bloodworm, blackworm, daphnia, or mosquito larvae in the evening. Fry from that same spawn took newly-hatched brine shrimp immediately with no special rearing food required. See our general feeding guide for broader conditioning advice ahead of breeding.

Temperament & Compatibility

The documented breeding account describes rubrolineata as hardy, undemanding, and notably less aggressive than many other Apistogramma species — a comparatively mild-mannered genus member rather than one requiring unusual caution with tankmates. That said, sexing the species is reportedly genuinely difficult (see below), which complicates stocking a confident pair or small group in the first place. No source checked for this guide documents specific tankmate recommendations beyond the general genus standard of avoiding aggressive or fin-nipping species.

A Genuinely Difficult Sexing Problem

Unlike most Apistogramma, where males and females are readily distinguished by size, fin extensions, and color, rubrolineata has a documented reputation for being hard to sex even for longtime keepers. A detailed apistogramma.com forum thread describes a trio kept for nearly a year with the sex ratio still unresolved — multiple experienced members, including well-known breeder Mike Wise, openly disagreed on how to tell the fish apart. One diagnostic eventually offered in that thread is a caudal-fin pattern present in males but absent in females, though the thread itself treats this as a working observation rather than a settled rule. If you're buying a "pair" or "trio" sold as already-sexed, treat that sexing as provisional and expect it may take longer than usual to confirm for yourself by behavior.

Breeding

The best documented breeding account for this species comes from a specific, dated import rather than a general reference work. The Potomac Valley Aquarium Society's October 2017 report describes a cave-spawning pattern: the female enters a clay-pot cave head-first to guard it, and turns bright yellow with black markings while brooding — a sharp, visible departure from her otherwise drab everyday coloring. The male, by contrast, patrols the outer territory and does not approach the nest itself during this period. That spawn produced roughly 40 fry, which took newly-hatched brine shrimp immediately with no transition food needed. Documented growth benchmarks from the same account: about 3/8 inch (around 1cm) by three weeks, and roughly 3/4 inch (around 2cm) by six weeks, with sparring and chasing behavior already established among the juveniles by that point — an early hint of the social hierarchy to plan tank space around. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process, and budget extra patience for confirming sexes before you expect a spawn at all.

Sourcing Notes

This is a genuine rarity in the hobby, not a species you should expect to find at general retail. German wholesale ornamental-fish exporter Aquarium Glaser — a specialist trade source, not a retail blog — profiles rubrolineata explicitly as one of the true rarities of the Apistogramma trade, noting its 2001 discovery and 2002 formal description and its limited, irregular export availability since. Plan on a wait list through a specialist importer or aquarium club rather than expecting walk-in availability, and when you do find it, expect the seller's sexing of any "pair" to be provisional given the documented difficulty above. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting specialist-import claims.

Final Thoughts

Rubrolineata is a quietly well-documented rarity: a dated, detailed breeding and fry-growth record exists despite how seldom the species reaches the trade, alongside two genuinely open questions worth knowing before you commit — a Bolivia-versus-Peru range discrepancy the formal literature hasn't caught up to, and a documented sexing problem that stumped multiple experienced forum members at once. If you want a better-documented, more available relative from roughly the same Bolivia/Madre-de-Dios border region to research alongside it, this site's own luelingi shares that general range, though the two aren't known to be close relatives and luelingi carries its own separate size and look-alike-variant questions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where does Apistogramma rubrolineata actually come from — Bolivia or Peru?

Both, with a genuine gap between the formal record and field reports. The 2002 original description (Hein, Zarske & Zapata) and FishBase both list the species as known only from its type locality: the Río Manuripi in Departamento Pando, Bolivia. But a detailed 2017 hobbyist collection report from the Potomac Valley Aquarium Society documents a second population from a creek called "Alegria," in the Madre de Dios drainage of Peru — hundreds of kilometers from the Bolivian type locality. No source checked for this guide formally reconciles the two; treat the Río Manuripi, Bolivia record as the only taxonomically confirmed locality and the Peruvian population as a credible but not yet formally described field report.

Is it hard to tell male and female Apistogramma rubrolineata apart?

Reportedly yes, even for experienced keepers. What water parameters does Apistogramma rubrolineata need?

Sources genuinely disagree, which is worth presenting as a range rather than a single number. The Peruvian wild-collection site (Alegria creek) was measured at 17ppm TDS, pH 6.5, and 75°F (24°C) in shallow, roughly 2-foot-deep clearwater over mud and gravel. The same keeper's home tank ran softer and warmer: pH 6, 60ppm TDS, about 80°F (27°C), filtered with a sponge filter and maintained with rainwater changes. A separate Bolivian-side forum breeder, closer to the species' Río Manuripi type locality, reported a markedly different setup — pH 4.5–5.5 and roughly 280ppm TDS. Until more reports narrow this down, treat pH 4.5–6.5 and a wide TDS range as the realistic working window rather than picking one report as definitive. Why is Apistogramma rubrolineata so hard to find for sale?
