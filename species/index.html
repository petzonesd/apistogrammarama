Apistogramma Species Library

Complete care profiles for every major dwarf cichlid species.

Apistogramma cacatuoides is the easiest species for a first tank; borellii and trifasciata suit a nano setup; nijsseni, steindachneri, and viejita are better second or third species once you've bred cacatuoides successfully. Water chemistry, tank size, temperament, and breeding behavior all vary by species — the comparison table below lines up the twenty-nine most commonly kept species side by side.

Each species has unique requirements. Water chemistry, tank size, temperament, breeding behavior — they all matter. Below are the guides to help you choose, set up, and succeed with the species that fits your setup.

The Essential Three

Apistogramma cacatuoides

Red-nosed Apisto (Cacatuoides)

The most popular, most forgiving, most widely available. Where most keepers start — and often where they stay.

Tank: 20g long min, pH: 5.5–6.8

Apistogramma borellii

Yellow Apisto (Borellii)

The smallest, most peaceful. Stunning in planted nano tanks. Can be territorial but rarely aggressive.

Tank: 15g min, pH: 5.5–7.2

Apistogramma nijsseni
Apistogramma nijsseni

Panda Dwarf Cichlid (Nijsseni)

Bold, interactive, and challenging. For keepers ready to move beyond cacatuoides. Excellent parents.

Tank: 20g min, pH: 5.0–6.5

Also Beginner-Friendly

Apistogramma trifasciata
Apistogramma trifasciata

Three-Striped Dwarf Cichlid (Trifasciata)

Borellii's closest rival for nano tanks — even smaller, just as tolerant of cool water, and one of the most adaptable apistos in the hobby.

Tank: 15g min • pH: 5.5–7.5

Apistogramma hoignei
Apistogramma hoignei

Hoigne's Dwarf Cichlid (Hoignei)

A wide-ranging Orinoco species with one of the broadest pH tolerances in the genus — a forgiving bridge toward blackwater specialists.

Tank: 20g long min • pH: 5.0–7.0

Intermediate Species

Agassiz's Dwarf Cichlid (Agassizii)

Widespread and highly variable by locality — red, blue, and fire-red strains. Larger than cacatuoides.

Tank: 20g, 24"+ long, pH: 5.5–7.0

Macmaster's Dwarf Cichlid (Macmasteri)

Red-faced and assertive — and unusually tolerant of harder tap water for this genus.

Tank: 20g min, pH: 5.5–7.0

Two-Striped Dwarf Cichlid (Bitaeniata)

Bigger and feistier than the beginner species, with a warm-water blackwater lean and a signature double stripe plus red-netted tail on mature males.

Tank: 20g, 24"+ long • pH: 5.0–6.5

Steindachner's Dwarf Cichlid (Steindachneri)

Bold, blunt-headed, and the largest species on this list. Needs real tank space.

Tank: 30g+, 40"+ long, pH: 5.5–7.0

Advanced & Rare Species

Viejita Apisto (Viejita)

Rare and delicate. Long-term SF, requires expertise. The ultimate collector's fish.

Hong's Dwarf Cichlid (Hongsloi)

Tiny, peaceful, understated beauty. For planted nano specialists.

Inca Dwarf Cichlid (Baenschi)

The most demanding water chemistry on this list — pH to 4.0, hardness to 1 dGH. Males flush deep red-orange under a dramatic sail-fin display.

Elizabeth's Dwarf Cichlid (Elizabethae)

A true Rio Negro blackwater specialist with red-and-blue facial markings on males — and a micropredator streak that rules out ornamental shrimp tankmates.

Panduro Dwarf Cichlid (Panduro)

Soft blue coloration and demanding blackwater chemistry to match baenschi and elizabethae. Nicknamed "blue panda" in the trade — unrelated to nijsseni's actual panda markings.

Apistogramma eunotus (Eunotus)

A genus outlier: large-bodied and genuinely milder-water-tolerant than its relatives, with a prized but inconsistent "orange-tailed" male color form. Specialist sourcing, not demanding chemistry.

Apistogramma eremnopyge

Genus-extreme softwater chemistry to match baenschi, plus a trade-name history (Strawberry, Red Beard, now "Diamond") that's confused buyers for over twenty years.

Apistogramma gibbiceps

One of the largest apistos in the genus, named for a "hump head" deformity its type specimen had and no other gibbiceps has ever shown. Rare beyond the wholesale trade — sourcing is the real challenge here, not chemistry.

Apistogramma barlowi

The genus's rule-breaker: one of only two known Apistogramma species that mouthbroods its eggs instead of guarding them in a cave. Demanding water chemistry plus specialist-only sourcing.

Apistogramma megastoma

The genus's other mouthbrooder — a maternal-only brooder formerly traded as "Kelleri" for nearly a decade after its 2017 description. Larger-bodied than barlowi, with sourcing just as specialist.

Apistogramma mendezi

Named for Chico Mendes, the Brazilian rainforest activist murdered in 1988 — a rare conservation story behind an apisto name. Extreme blackwater chemistry and one of the harder species on this list to breed.

Apistogramma diplotaenia

The Doublebanded Dwarf Cichlid — in the hobby since 1981 without ever becoming common. Its name essentially translates to bitaeniata's "two-striped," but they're unrelated species. Rare, expensive, and documented by only a handful of breeders.

Apistogramma iniridae

The first species on this site with a formal IUCN Vulnerable listing. Males show dramatic sailfin dorsal displays and orange or blue color variants side by side. Moderate water chemistry — the real challenge is finding wholesale-only stock, and not confusing it with its lyre-tailed cousin, uaupesi.

Apistogramma uaupesi

Iniridae's lyre-tailed cousin and the genus's most frequent mislabeling match — cichlidae.com groups both species together taxonomically. Genus-extreme blackwater chemistry (pH to 4.0) plus wholesale-only sourcing make this one of the more demanding species on this list.

Apistogramma pertensis

One of the most widely distributed Apistogramma species in the wild — spanning a long stretch of the Rio Negro and Amazon-Solimões mainstem — yet genuinely rare in the aquarium trade. Mild, moderate water chemistry; sourcing is the real challenge.

Apistogramma norberti

Traded for years as "großmaul" (bigmouth) before its 1991 description, with a taxonomic placement still unsettled between the cacatuoides and nijsseni species groups. Males carry a one-of-a-kind black dorsal spot and rounded tail. Genuinely rare — wholesale and specialist channels only.

Apistogramma pantalone

The only apisto on this site named after a fictional character — the Commedia dell'Arte merchant Pantalone, for the species' stiff, clumsy courtship display. A taxonomic cousin of nijsseni from fast-flowing Peruvian streams rather than still blackwater. Essentially undocumented in the trade.

Apistogramma cinilabra

Known from a single forest lake under one hectare near Iquitos, Peru — discovered in 2010 and flagged "potentially endangered" by the scientists who described it the following year, with logging threatening the surrounding forest. Males show genus-unique ash-grey lips. It's unclear if this species has ever been commercially exported for the hobby at all.

Apistogramma pleurotaenia

One of the oldest formally described species on this site (1909) — yet its original description placed its origin in Argentina, an error traced to mislabeled aquarium specimens and only corrected by research in 2024. No established common name, and genuinely scarce in the modern hobby despite over a century in the scientific record.

Apistogramma gephyra

Named by Kullander as a taxonomic "bridge" between agassizii and pertensis — a connection a leading reference curator now says "no longer holds." Juveniles are also mistaken for elizabethae, making this the most identification-tangled species on this list. Occasional wholesale import only.

Choosing Your Species

First apisto? Start with cacatuoides. Hardy, widely available, forgiving of beginner mistakes, and beautiful enough to enjoy for years.

Second or third? Try borellii or trifasciata if you love planted nano tanks, hoignei if you want a forgiving step toward blackwater species, or nijsseni if you're ready for personality and breeding.

Experienced breeder? Explore the rarer species — macmasteri, bitaeniata, viejita, hongsloi, baenschi, elizabethae, panduro, eunotus, eremnopyge, gibbiceps, barlowi, megastoma, mendezi, diplotaenia, iniridae, uaupesi, pertensis, norberti, pantalone, cinilabra, pleurotaenia, gephyra. Each has its own quirks and challenges.

Not All Apistos Are Equal

Wild-caught, F1, and captive-bred fish behave differently. Strain lines matter. Regional variants have different colors and sizes. Throughout these guides, we'll address where these differences come from and why they matter for keeping and breeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Apistogramma species is best for beginners?

Apistogramma cacatuoides. It tolerates a wider range of water chemistry than most apistos, is widely available, and breeds readily in a 20-gallon long.

How many Apistogramma species are there?

Over 90 species have been described, with more identified regularly. This site covers twenty-nine of the most commonly kept in the aquarium hobby, from beginner-friendly cacatuoides and trifasciata to advanced blackwater specialists like baenschi, elizabethae, and eremnopyge.

Which Apistogramma species works in a nano tank?

Apistogramma borellii and trifasciata are the best fits. Both run smaller and calmer than most of the genus, and a single pair of either can work in a heavily planted 10 to 15 gallon tank.

What is the hardest Apistogramma species to keep?

Baenschi and elizabethae need the most extreme water chemistry on this list (pH down to 4.0, hardness down to 1 dGH). Nijsseni and steindachneri also demand strict blackwater conditions and are unforgiving of parameter drift. Viejita is difficult for a different reason: genuine specimens are rare and frequently mislabeled in the trade.
