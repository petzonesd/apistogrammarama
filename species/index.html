Apistogramma Species Library

Complete care profiles for every major dwarf cichlid species.

Apistogramma cacatuoides is the easiest species for a first tank; borellii and trifasciata suit a nano setup; nijsseni, steindachneri, and viejita are better second or third species once you've bred cacatuoides successfully. Water chemistry, tank size, temperament, and breeding behavior all vary by species — the comparison table below lines up the nine most commonly kept species side by side.

Each species has unique requirements. Water chemistry, tank size, temperament, breeding behavior — they all matter. Below are the guides to help you choose, set up, and succeed with the species that fits your setup.

The Essential Three

Apistogramma cacatuoides

Red-nosed Apisto (Cacatuoides)

The most popular, most forgiving, most widely available. Where most keepers start — and often where they stay.

Tank: 20g long min • pH: 5.5–6.8

Apistogramma borellii
Apistogramma borellii

Yellow Apisto (Borellii)

The smallest, most peaceful. Stunning in planted nano tanks. Can be territorial but rarely aggressive.

Tank: 15g min • pH: 5.5–7.2

Apistogramma nijsseni
Apistogramma nijsseni

Panda Dwarf Cichlid (Nijsseni)

Bold, interactive, and challenging. For keepers ready to move beyond cacatuoides. Excellent parents.

Tank: 20g min • pH: 5.0–6.5

Also Beginner-Friendly

Apistogramma trifasciata
Apistogramma trifasciata

Three-Striped Dwarf Cichlid (Trifasciata)

Borellii's closest rival for nano tanks — even smaller, just as tolerant of cool water, and one of the most adaptable apistos in the hobby.

Tank: 15g min • pH: 5.5–7.5

Intermediate Species

Agassiz's Dwarf Cichlid (Agassizii)

Widespread and highly variable by locality — red, blue, and fire-red strains. Larger than cacatuoides.

Tank: 20g, 24"+ long • pH: 5.5–7.0

Macmaster's Dwarf Cichlid (Macmasteri)

Red-faced and assertive — and unusually tolerant of harder tap water for this genus.

Tank: 20g min • pH: 5.5–7.0

Steindachner's Dwarf Cichlid (Steindachneri)

Bold, blunt-headed, and the largest species on this list. Needs real tank space.

Tank: 30g+, 40"+ long • pH: 5.5–7.0

Advanced & Rare Species

Viejita Apisto (Viejita)

Rare and delicate. Long-term SF, requires expertise. The ultimate collector's fish.

Hong's Dwarf Cichlid (Hongsloi)

Tiny, peaceful, understated beauty. For planted nano specialists.

Choosing Your Species

First apisto? Start with cacatuoides. Hardy, widely available, forgiving of beginner mistakes, and beautiful enough to enjoy for years.

Second or third? Try borellii or trifasciata if you love planted nano tanks, or nijsseni if you're ready for personality and breeding.

Experienced breeder? Explore the rarer species — macmasteri, viejita, hongsloi. Each has its own quirks and challenges.

Not All Apistos Are Equal

Wild-caught, F1, and captive-bred fish behave differently. Strain lines matter. Regional variants have different colors and sizes. Throughout these guides, we'll address where these differences come from and why they matter for keeping and breeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Apistogramma species is best for beginners?

Apistogramma cacatuoides. It tolerates a wider range of water chemistry than most apistos, is widely available, and breeds readily in a 20-gallon long.

How many Apistogramma species are there?

Over 90 species have been described, with more identified regularly. This site covers the nine most commonly kept in the aquarium hobby, from beginner-friendly cacatuoides and trifasciata to advanced blackwater specialists.

Which Apistogramma species works in a nano tank?

Apistogramma borellii and trifasciata are the best fits. Both run smaller and calmer than most of the genus, and a single pair of either can work in a heavily planted 10 to 15 gallon tank.

What is the hardest Apistogramma species to keep?

Nijsseni and steindachneri demand the strictest blackwater conditions and are the least forgiving of parameter drift. Viejita is difficult for a different reason: genuine specimens are rare and frequently mislabeled in the trade.
