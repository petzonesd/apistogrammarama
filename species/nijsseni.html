Apistogramma nijsseni

Panda Dwarf Cichlid

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Male to 3.1" (8 cm), female to 2.4" (6 cm)
  • Tank: 20 gallons minimum
  • pH: 5.0–6.5
  • Temperature: 73–82°F (23–28°C)
  • Hardness: Very soft, 1–8 dGH
  • Temperament: Peaceful, but choosy about mates and territorial when breeding
  • Difficulty: Intermediate to advanced

Overview

Apistogramma nijsseni — the panda dwarf cichlid — comes from the blackwater tributaries of the Ucayali River basin in Peru, near Iquitos: tannin-stained, forest-shaded creeks with a soft leaf-litter bottom. Described by Kullander in 1979 and named for ichthyologist Han Nijssen, it's one of the more recently introduced Apistogramma species to the hobby, and one of the more visually distinct.

The sexes look like they belong to different species. Males run larger, with a blue sheen along the flanks, a red-edged rounded tail, and elongated dorsal rays in full display. Females turn a striking bright yellow with bold black blotches when guarding a brood — the "panda" marking the common name refers to. Outside of breeding condition, females are more subdued but still carry a diagnostic dark eye spot.

Tank Setup

Recreate the blackwater habitat as closely as you can — this species is noticeably less forgiving of hard, alkaline water than cacatuoides or borellii.

  • Substrate: Fine sand for sifting.
  • Leaf litter: Not optional here. A real leaf litter layer (Indian almond, oak, or beech) drives tannins down toward the low pH this species expects, and doubles as microfauna habitat for fry.
  • Hardscape: Driftwood, root tangles, and dense low planting. Visual barriers matter — females stake out individual territories and need places to disappear from each other.
  • Filtration: Gentle flow, but don't skip on capacity — nijsseni is unusually sensitive to nitrate buildup for an apisto, and needs consistent water changes.
  • Cover: A tight-fitting lid. This species can and will jump.

Feeding

Not a picky eater — nijsseni takes flake, pellet, and live or frozen foods readily, and will pick at the substrate throughout the day. Live foods measurably improve color and breeding condition, as with most apistos. Feed small amounts multiple times daily rather than one large feeding.

Temperament & Compatibility

Peaceful for a cichlid, and genuinely interactive — this is a fish that will watch you back through the glass. Pairs bond closely, and can be particular about who they'll spawn with; putting together "just any" male and female doesn't guarantee a match the way it might with cacatuoides. Keep either a single bonded pair or, in a larger tank, one male with several females.

Good tankmates: corydoras, hatchetfish, pencilfish, small tetras, and other peaceful surface or mid-water fish. Avoid mixing with other Apistogramma species, which raises both territorial conflict and hybridization risk.

Breeding

Conditioning

Breeding success starts with a genuinely bonded pair — don't force it. Once paired, condition heavily on live foods and hold water soft and acidic; this species has historically shown skewed male-heavy fry ratios when bred too warm, so keep temperatures moderate rather than pushing heat to speed things along.

Spawning

A cave spawner, typically in a small crevice or coconut hut. The female takes on aggressive, near-total guard duty over the eggs and fry; the male holds the surrounding territory. Expect the female to become highly intolerant of tankmates during this window.

Fry Care

Eggs hatch in roughly 2–3 days; fry are free-swimming within about a week. Start on infusoria or very fine fry food, moving to newly hatched brine shrimp as they grow. This species is known for good parental care — a well-matched pair will raise a large fraction of the brood without much intervention, provided water quality stays high.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Because pairs can be selective about mates, buying an already-bonded pair (rather than assembling one yourself from unrelated individuals) meaningfully improves your odds of a successful spawn. Both tank-bred and, less often, wild imports from the Ucayali basin show up in the trade. Check our sourcing guide before you buy.

Final Thoughts

Nijsseni is the character on this list — visually dramatic, genuinely interactive, and a real step up in difficulty from cacatuoides or borellii. It rewards keepers willing to commit to soft, acidic, blackwater conditions and a little patience finding the right pair. Not a first apisto, but an excellent second or third.
