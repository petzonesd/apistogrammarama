Apistogramma agassizii

Agassiz's Dwarf Cichlid

By Pet Zone SD Team • September 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Male to 3.5" (9 cm), female to 2" (5 cm)
  • Tank: 20 gallons minimum, 24"+ long
  • pH: 5.5–7.0
  • Temperature: 74–82°F (23–28°C)
  • Hardness: Soft, 1–10 dGH
  • Temperament: Peaceful toward other species, territorial toward its own
  • Difficulty: Intermediate

Overview

One of the most widespread Apistogramma species in the wild, found from Peru through Brazil to the Amazon delta — which also makes it one of the most variable. "Agassizii" on a fish tag can mean wildly different things depending on which locality it traces back to: Alenquer red tail, fire red, double red, white seam, and more, each a distinct regional color form rather than a separate species.

What they share is a distinctive spade-shaped caudal fin and a diagnostic black lateral stripe running the length of the body. Males are slender and elongated with intense red, orange, or blue coloration depending on strain, and noticeably longer finnage than females. This is also one of the larger commonly-kept apistos — males can push 3.5 inches, meaningfully bigger than a cacatuoides.

Tank Setup

  • Substrate: Fine, smooth sand. Agassizii is a dedicated substrate-sifter and will spend much of its day working through it for food.
  • Leaf litter: Recommended, not mandatory — this species ranges across clear-, black-, and whitewater habitats in the wild, so it's more flexible than true blackwater specialists.
  • Hardscape: Driftwood, dense planting, floating cover to diffuse light. This species prefers dim, subdued lighting over a bright tank.
  • Filtration: Gentle flow — sponge filters or a reduced-flow hang-on-back. Strong current stresses them.
  • Tank size: Because of their larger adult size, give a pair more room than you would cacatuoides — 24 inches of length minimum, more for a harem.

Feeding

An unfussy omnivore that takes quality flake, pellet, and live or frozen bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia readily. Live foods bring out the full intensity of red and blue strains in particular. Feed small portions two to three times daily.

Temperament & Compatibility

Peaceful with other species, territorial with its own — the standard apisto pattern, but agassizii's larger size means disputes carry a bit more weight than with cacatuoides or borellii. Best kept as a single male with two or three females rather than a simple pair; that spreads out his attention and reduces the chance any one female gets harassed.

Compatible with tetras, corydoras, and other peaceful community fish that stay out of the bottom territory it claims. As with all apistos, dwarf shrimp are at risk once the fish reach adult size.

Breeding

Conditioning

Condition on live foods for two to three weeks before expecting a spawn. Stable, soft, slightly acidic water helps, though agassizii is more tolerant of parameter drift than blackwater specialists like nijsseni.

Spawning

A cave spawner. The female takes primary responsibility for the eggs, laid on a cave ceiling or wall, while the male defends the surrounding territory from intruders.

Fry Care

Eggs hatch in about 2–3 days, with fry free-swimming within a week. Start on infusoria or crushed fry food, moving up to newly hatched brine shrimp. Keep water quality high — this species does not tolerate elevated nitrates any better than other apistos, regardless of how forgiving it is about hardness.

Variants & Locality Forms

Because agassizii's range is so large, locality and strain matter more here than with most apistos. "Alenquer red tail," "fire red," "double red," and "white seam" are among the more common named forms in the trade — each bred for a specific fin or body color rather than representing a different species. If you plan to breed true to a given form, buy from a source that can tell you the locality or line, and don't mix forms in the same tank.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Given how many color forms circulate under the same species name, ask specifically which strain or locality you're buying — a reputable seller will know. Look for straight spines, undamaged spade tails, and active foraging behavior at the substrate. Visit our sourcing guide for more on vetting sellers.

Final Thoughts

Agassizii rewards keepers who want size and color variety without stepping up to a genuinely difficult blackwater species. It's more tolerant of real-world tap water than nijsseni, more visually varied than borellii, and a natural next step once cacatuoides feels too easy.
