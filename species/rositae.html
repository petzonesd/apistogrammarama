Apistogramma rositae

Apistogramma rositae

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 1.6" (4.0cm SL) for males per FishBase; no female figure found in any source checked
  • Tank: Not documented; genus-standard 15–20g default by body size
  • pH: Original description points to cooler clear-water streams; one 8th/9th-generation captive line is maintained at pH 5.4
  • Temperature: One documented captive line kept at ~77°F (25°C); wild habitat described only as "cooler"
  • Hardness: One documented captive line kept at conductivity ~125 µS/cm²; no wild-water figures published
  • Temperament: A documented 1-male/2-female trio showed real female-on-female aggression
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genuine trade rarity, thinly and sometimes conflictingly documented

Overview

Apistogramma rositae is a genus rarity whose own name points straight at one of this site's better-known species. Uwe Römer, Ingo Römer, and Ingo Hahn described it in 2006, naming it for Rosita Bonhaus, longtime partner of Hans-Albrecht Baensch, publisher of the Baensch Aquarium Atlas. The original description doesn't stop at the dedication — it explicitly states the name also highlights the similarity and apparent close systematic links between rositae and this site's own A. baenschi, named for Baensch himself. That's a documented taxonomic relationship, not just a coincidence of naming.

The relationship isn't unanimous, though. A hobbyist maintaining a long-running captive line instead describes rositae as belonging to the nijsseni species-group by appearance and behavior — a real disagreement between the original description's formal placement and at least one experienced keeper's own classification, and this guide reports both rather than picking a side. FishBase lists the species as IUCN Data Deficient, assessed in 2014, and gives a male maximum size of 4.0cm (1.6in) standard length, citing Römer's 2007 Cichlid Atlas Vol. 2; no source checked here gives a corresponding female figure.

Natural Habitat

The type locality is an igarapé — a small forest creek — in the Río Marañón drainage between the towns of Nauta and Barranca, roughly opposite the mouth of the Río Huallaga, in Peru's Loreto Department. That puts it in the same broad northern-Peru river system as several other Apistogramma this site covers, though not the same specific drainage as baenschi's Shanushi/Paranapura habitat. The original description characterizes the species' habitat as cooler, clear forest water — a notable departure from the warm blackwater most hobbyists associate with Peruvian apistos, and worth flagging precisely because it cuts against the genus-standard assumption.

Tank Setup

No source checked for this guide documents a tested aquarium setup in the kind of detail this site provides for better-known species — rositae is simply too rare in the hobby for that to exist yet. What follows leans on the one detailed captive-line account available plus genus-standard defaults.

  • Tank size: Not documented species-specifically. Given its small adult size, a 15–20 gallon setup is a reasonable genus-standard starting point. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: Not documented species-specifically, but a forest-stream origin points toward a soft substrate with leaf litter and driftwood over an open, rocky layout.
  • Water chemistry: A documented eighth- or ninth-generation captive line is maintained at pH 5.4 and conductivity ~125 µS/cm², standard soft-acidic-water territory for the genus — even though the original wild-habitat description characterizes the source water only as "cooler" and clear, without published chemistry figures. See our water parameters guide.
  • Filtration & flow: Not documented. A gentle-flow default is reasonable given the species' forest-stream origin.

Feeding

No species-specific feeding account was found in any source checked for this guide. Follow genus-standard practice: a quality sinking flake or micro-pellet as a daily base, supplemented with live or frozen foods like baby brine shrimp and bloodworm for conditioning and color. See our general feeding guide for broader strategy.

Temperament & Compatibility

Documentation here is sparse but specific rather than generic. A 2011 hobbyist forum account of a one-male, two-female trio in Warsaw documents real aggression between the two females, including one chasing the other while the chased female displayed spawning coloration — a concrete data point on how a small group can behave, not a blanket difficulty rating. No broader compatibility data (tankmates, minimum group dynamics beyond this one trio) was found. As with every species on this site, never house multiple Apistogramma species together given the hybridization risk.

Breeding

Rositae has a real, if scattered, captive-breeding record that its own reference listings don't reflect. Aquatic Republic's species page lists breeding as unreported — the same database-silence pattern this site has flagged on other rare Apistogramma. The real picture is better documented: a hobbyist in Lyon, France posted in 2014 about maintaining a captive line descended from fish imported in 2008, by then into roughly its eighth or ninth generation — a genuinely long-running bloodline for a species this obscure. Separately, a December 2025 apistogramma.com forum thread describes a hobbyist's newly acquired wild-caught pair producing two fry batches within a month of purchase (the first batch was lost, the second survived), confirming the species is still an active wild import as well as an established captive-bred fish. Neither account gives egg counts or fry-rearing specifics beyond the fact of successful spawning. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process until more detail is published.

Sourcing Notes

Every source checked for this guide describes rositae as a genuine trade rarity, sourced wild rather than farmed, with the December 2025 forum thread confirming wild imports are still reaching hobbyists now. Given the real disagreement over whether the species belongs with baenschi or with the nijsseni group by appearance, ask a seller for the specific collection locality (Río Marañón between Nauta and Barranca is the documented type locality) rather than relying on the species name alone to confirm identification. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's identification claims.

Final Thoughts

Rositae is a specialist's fish twice over: rare enough that basic facts like female size remain undocumented, yet carrying a real, citable taxonomic story — a name that formally ties it to this site's own baenschi, and a captive-breeding record that quietly outpaces what the reference databases admit to. If you want a far better-documented, far more available entry point into the genus first, this site's own cacatuoides is the better starting place. If the appeal is precisely in a species this thinly recorded, rositae rewards the kind of careful, source-by-source research this guide has tried to model.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Apistogramma rositae named after?

Rosita Bonhaus, the long-term partner of Hans-Albrecht Baensch, publisher of the Baensch Aquarium Atlas series. Uwe Römer, Ingo Römer, and Ingo Hahn described the species in 2006. Their original description states the name also highlights the similarity and apparent close systematic links between A. rositae and this site's own A. baenschi — a species named for Baensch himself.

What water parameters does Apistogramma rositae need?

Sources genuinely disagree. The original 2006 description characterizes its wild habitat as cooler, clear forest streams, distinct from the warm blackwater most apistos are associated with. A documented hobbyist account of an eighth- or ninth-generation captive line, by contrast, reports standard warm-acidic-softwater parameters: about 25°C (77°F), pH 5.4, and conductivity around 125 µS/cm². Both are real, sourced figures describing the same species at different points — wild habitat versus a long-established aquarium bloodline — rather than a single confirmed range.

Has Apistogramma rositae been bred in captivity?

Yes, repeatedly, despite reference databases suggesting otherwise. Aquatic Republic's species page lists breeding as unreported, but a hobbyist in Lyon, France documented maintaining a captive line through roughly its eighth or ninth generation by 2014, descended from fish imported in 2008. More recently, a December 2025 apistogramma.com forum thread describes a newly acquired wild-caught pair producing two fry batches within a month of purchase, so the species remains both an active wild import and an established (if rare) captive-bred fish.

How big does Apistogramma rositae get, and is it aggressive?

FishBase reports a male maximum of 4.0cm (1.6in) standard length; no source checked for this guide gives a specific female figure, so it's reported here as unreported rather than guessed. On temperament, a 2011 forum account of a one-male, two-female trio documented real aggression between the two females, including one chasing the other while displaying spawning coloration — a genuine, specific data point on intra-group dynamics rather than a general difficulty rating.
