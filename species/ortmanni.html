Apistogramma ortmanni

Apistogramma ortmanni

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Not documented precisely; described as slightly more slender-bodied than regani
  • Tank: Not documented; genus-standard 15–20g used as a working default
  • pH: ~6.0 general care; low 6s for breeding
  • Hardness: Under 5°dGH general; KH reduced to 1–2° for breeding
  • Temperature: Not documented species-specifically; genus-standard 73–82°F used as a working default
  • Temperament: Not documented; keep overall cichlid-tank load moderate
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genuinely uncommon, and what's sold under its name is often a different species

Overview

Apistogramma ortmanni is one of this genus's oldest formally described species, and one of its most frequently mislabeled in the opposite direction from the usual pattern. Carl H. Eigenmann described it in 1912, from a range running along the eastern Venezuela/Guyana border into Suriname. Rather than ortmanni itself being sold under a disguise, longtime breeders Mike and Diane Wise, writing on the hobbyist reference site The Krib, document that fish commercially sold as "Apistogramma ortmanni" are often actually this site's own A. steindachneri — a more available, frequently-bred species standing in for a genuinely scarce one.

That scarcity is largely geographic and logistical: ortmanni's native range sees reduced fish exports generally, which keeps real specimens uncommon in the trade regardless of demand. If you specifically want ortmanni rather than steindachneri sold under its name, the caudal-peduncle marking is the detail to check — see Sourcing Notes below.

Natural Habitat

The Krib places ortmanni's native range along the eastern Venezuela/Guyana border, extending into Suriname. Unlike many apistos on this site, it isn't a true blackwater specialist — it's documented in clear-water streams, though soft and acidic conditions are still recommended for keeping and especially breeding it.

Feeding

No source checked for this guide gives ortmanni-specific feeding guidance. Follow the genus-standard approach used elsewhere on this site: small sinking foods as a daily base diet, with live or frozen bloodworm and brine shrimp for conditioning before breeding. See our general feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

No source checked for this guide rates ortmanni's temperament in detail, but one real-world data point is worth passing on: The Krib documents a case of unexpected aggression in a 40-gallon community tank mixing ortmanni with rams, rummy-nose tetras, and other apistos, with the overall cichlid stocking density suspected as the cause rather than ortmanni specifically. Keep the total cichlid load moderate rather than assuming any one species is the problem if aggression shows up in a mixed tank.

Breeding

Ortmanni is a cave spawner, consistent with every other species on this site. The Krib reports it as easier to breed than its close relative A. regani, with soft, acidic water — KH reduced to 1–2° and pH in the low 6s — recommended specifically to encourage spawning. No detailed fry-care timeline specific to this species was found in the sources checked for this guide. Follow our general breeding guide for the broader conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

Ortmanni has a documented mislabeling problem that runs the opposite direction from most cases on this site: instead of the real species being sold under a placeholder name, a more common substitute is sold under its name. The Krib states plainly that fish commercially labeled "Apistogramma ortmanni" are often actually this site's own A. steindachneri. The detail to check is the caudal-peduncle spot: genuine ortmanni shows an oval spot covering roughly two-thirds of the peduncle's height with fainter tail bands that don't reach the fin edges, while steindachneri's spot runs smaller. If a seller can't speak to that distinction, or the stock's origin doesn't trace back to the eastern Venezuela/Guyana/Suriname border region, treat the identification skeptically. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Ortmanni rewards the kind of buyer who likes untangling a genuinely confused trade history as much as keeping the fish itself. It's over a century old as a formal description, genuinely scarce due to export patterns rather than rarity in the wild, and routinely confused with this site's own steindachneri — in the direction of steindachneri standing in for it, not the reverse. If you can confirm the caudal-peduncle marking and the stock's regional origin, you're ahead of most casual buyers in this corner of the genus.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Apistogramma ortmanni commonly mislabeled in the trade?

Yes, and it runs in the opposite direction from most mislabeling cases on this site: rather than ortmanni being sold under another name, fish sold under the name "Apistogramma ortmanni" are, according to longtime breeders Mike and Diane Wise writing on The Krib, often actually this site's own A. steindachneri. The two are told apart by the caudal-peduncle spot: ortmanni's covers roughly two-thirds of the peduncle's height in an oval shape with fainter tail bands that don't reach the fin edges, while steindachneri's spot is smaller. Genuine ortmanni is uncommon in the hobby, partly because of its limited native range and reduced export volume from that region — which is exactly the kind of gap a mislabeled, more available substitute tends to fill.

Who described Apistogramma ortmanni, and where is it from?

Carl H. Eigenmann described it in 1912. Its native range runs from the eastern Venezuela/Guyana border to Suriname, in clear-water streams rather than the classic tannin-stained blackwater some other apistos favor.

What water parameters does Apistogramma ortmanni need?

Not a true blackwater species, but still wanting soft, acidic conditions. The Krib recommends hardness under 5°dGH and a pH around 6.0 for general care, narrowing further for breeding: KH reduced to 1–2° and pH in the low 6s. See our water parameters guide for building soft, slightly acidic water safely.

Is Apistogramma ortmanni easy to breed?

Reportedly easier than its close relative A. regani, according to The Krib, with soft, acidic water recommended to encourage spawning. No source checked for this guide gives a detailed fry-care timeline specific to this species.

How do you tell Apistogramma ortmanni apart from A. regani?

By body shape and the caudal-peduncle marking. The Krib describes ortmanni as slightly more slender-bodied than regani, with an oval caudal-peduncle spot covering about two-thirds of the peduncle's height and fainter tail bands that don't reach the fin edges — regani instead shows a full-height stripe across the peduncle.
