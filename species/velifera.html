Apistogramma velifera

Apistogramma velifera

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: ~2.8" (7cm) male, ~1.6" (4cm) female — larger than most apistos on this site
  • Tank: Not documented precisely; genus-standard 20g+ given its larger-than-average size
  • pH: ~5.0 native; tolerant of some deviation per commercial guidance
  • Temperature: Not documented species-specifically; genus-standard 73–82°F used as a working default
  • Hardness: Below 1°dH native
  • Temperament: Males fairly aggressive toward other males; some paternal fry care reported
  • Difficulty: Advanced — never regularly imported; specialist-bred stock only

Overview

Apistogramma velifera is one of the genus's most visually dramatic courtship performers. Wolfgang Staeck formally described it in 2003 in Cichliden Lexikon, Teil 3, giving it a species name — velifera, "bearing a sail" — that describes exactly what a displaying male does: he swims sideways toward a female while gradually unfolding his dorsal fin, then turns increasingly head-down until he's nearly vertical in the water with the fin fully spread, like a ship running up its sail. Before the formal description, the fish had already built a following in the trade under names describing the same display — "Segelflossen" (German for sailfin), "Vierstreifen" (four stripes), and a locality name, "Puerto Ayacucho."

Staeck's name shows up elsewhere on this site. This site's own A. staecki was named in his honor in 1985, after he collected its type specimens on a Bolivian expedition, and he returned in 2008 as co-describer of this site's own A. erythrura. Velifera is a third credit for the same taxonomist in this site's library — he's the person who first gave this fish, and its distinctive display, a formal scientific identity.

Natural Habitat

Aquarium Glaser, a German wholesale importer, does not give a confirmed native range for velifera beyond noting "Puerto Ayacucho" as an early trade name rather than a confirmed type locality — treat that as a lead, not a settled fact. The same source describes velifera's wild water as very soft and acidic, hardness below 1°dH and pH around 5, and notes it's sometimes found alongside wild Altum angelfish, a classic indicator of genuinely soft, acidic South American blackwater.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: Not documented precisely in the sources checked for this guide. Given its larger-than-average adult size (up to 7cm for males) and the male-to-male aggression noted below, a 20-gallon-plus tank is a safer working default than the genus-standard minimum used for smaller species on this site. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: No species-specific decor guidance exists in the sources checked for this guide. Open swimming space matters here more than for most apistos, given the male's display behavior — avoid over-cluttering the tank to the point a displaying male has no room to perform.
  • Water chemistry: Wild water is very soft and acidic (below 1°dH, pH around 5), but Aquarium Glaser specifically describes the species as not very demanding in aquarium conditions — some tolerance for less extreme water appears real. See our water parameters guide for building soft, acidic water safely regardless.
  • Filtration & flow: Not documented for this species specifically. Gentle flow and stable, mature water are the safe genus-wide default used elsewhere on this site — see our water quality guide.

Feeding

No source checked for this guide gives velifera-specific feeding guidance. Follow the genus-standard approach used elsewhere on this site: small sinking foods as a daily base diet, with live or frozen bloodworm and brine shrimp for conditioning before breeding. See our general feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

Aquarium Glaser specifically notes that males are fairly aggressive toward other males — a real consideration if you're hoping to keep more than one male in the same tank, especially during the display and spawning period described above. On the more reassuring side, the same source reports that males sometimes help care for the offspring, which isn't the universal pattern across this genus and is worth factoring into a breeding setup.

Breeding

Velifera is a cave spawner, consistent with every other species on this site, with the male's dramatic sail display serving as the courtship signal before spawning. Unlike most apistos on this site, Aquarium Glaser reports that males sometimes participate in offspring care rather than leaving it entirely to the female — worth watching for if you're trying to document breeding behavior firsthand, since it isn't the universal genus pattern. No specific breeding-tank water parameters beyond the general wild-habitat figures above were found in the sources checked for this guide. Follow our general breeding guide for the broader conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

Velifera is a genuine specialist fish, not a store-shelf species. Aquarium Glaser states plainly that it has never been imported regularly and that no current wild imports appear to exist — what's available comes from German-bred, tank-raised stock through specialist channels rather than a steady supply chain. If you're offered velifera, expect to be buying captive-bred fish from a breeder or specialist importer, and treat any claim of fresh wild-import stock with real skepticism given this documented pattern. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Velifera earns its place on this site for the display alone — few apistos put on as visually dramatic a courtship performance as a male fully unfurling that oversized dorsal fin. It's not an easy species to source, and documentation beyond the basics is thin, but it adds a third credited species from Wolfgang Staeck to this site's library, alongside the man his own staecki profile honors and the erythrura description he co-authored.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Apistogramma velifera called the "sailfin" apisto?

For the male's courtship display. Aquarium Glaser documents males displaying to females by swimming sideways while gradually unfolding the dorsal fin, eventually turning nearly head-down and spreading the fin fully, sometimes holding almost vertical in the water — the fin reads like an unfurled sail at full extension. The Latin species name velifera literally means "bearing a sail." Before its formal description, the fish circulated in the trade under informal names reflecting the same feature and its collection point: Apistogramma sp. "Segelflossen" (German for sailfin), sp. "Puerto Ayacucho," and sp. "Vierstreifen" (four stripes).

Who described Apistogramma velifera, and is there a connection to other species on this site?

Wolfgang Staeck formally described it in 2003, in Cichliden Lexikon, Teil 3: Südamerikanische Zwergbuntbarsche (Dähne Verlag). Staeck has a direct connection to two other species already on this site: this site's own A. staecki was named in his honor in 1985 after he collected its type specimens, and he returned 23 years later as co-describer of this site's own A. erythrura. Velifera adds a third entry to that same taxonomist's body of work represented here.

How big does Apistogramma velifera get?

On the larger side for this genus. Aquarium Glaser gives roughly 7cm for males and 4cm for females, with available stock sized from about 4 to 7cm — close to this site's own A. kullanderi's female size, though well short of kullanderi's record-setting male length.

What water parameters does Apistogramma velifera need?

Aquarium Glaser describes its wild water as very soft and acidic — hardness below 1°dH and a pH around 5 — but also calls the species not especially demanding in aquarium conditions, meaning some deviation from those extremes is tolerated. See our water parameters guide for building soft, acidic water safely.

Is Apistogramma velifera easy to find in the hobby?

No — Aquarium Glaser states plainly that the species has never been imported regularly and that no current wild imports appear to exist; what's available is German-bred, tank-raised stock from specialist breeders rather than a steady wild-collected supply. Budget for a specialist search rather than a local pet store.
