Apistogramma helkeri

Apistogramma helkeri

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 1.2–1.6" (3–4cm SL), among the smallest on this site
  • Tank: 16g (60L) minimum, group of 2+
  • pH: 5.5–6.5
  • Temperature: 73–79°F (23–26°C)
  • Hardness: 1–5 dGH native; ~5 dH for breeding
  • Temperament: Calm but territorial; aggressive toward conspecifics and similar-looking apistos
  • Difficulty: Advanced — rare, breeding-club-only sourcing and no documented breeding report

Overview

Apistogramma helkeri is a tiny, recently described Orinoco dwarf cichlid best known for how closely it resembles this site's own A. hongsloi. Ingo Schindler and Wolfgang Staeck formally described it in 2013 in Vertebrate Zoology (63(3):301–306), naming hongsloi directly in their diagnosis as the species it's "most similar to." The two are told apart on coloration and dorsal fin shape rather than anything dramatic: helkeri carries a rounded, hyaline, unmarked caudal fin and short, truncate dorsal fin lappets without extensions, a large vertically extended caudal spot, a chainlike or zigzag lateral band reaching the 7th bar but stopping short of the caudal fin and spot, an upper pectoral spot, and three distinct abdominal stripes. FishBase's English common name for the species, "Roundfinned dwarf cichlid," points at the same rounded-fin trait used to separate it from hongsloi. On the PlanetCatfish forum, moderator Mike Wise sharpened the comparison further, noting helkeri resembles several specific hongsloi populations (the site's own A113, A114, and A115 entries) and that an even, non-serrated dorsal fin is the trait unique to helkeri's complex — a genuine identification note for anyone offered a "hongsloi" from its native range.

This site hasn't been able to independently confirm the etymology behind the species epithet "helkeri" — the original description's full text sits behind a subscription wall, and no secondary source checked for this guide states who or what the name honors. Rather than guess, this guide leaves that detail open rather than inventing an answer.

Natural Habitat

Helkeri's type locality is the lower Río Cuao drainage in Estado Amazonas, Venezuela, part of the wider Orinoco drainage. FishBase records it as a blackwater-swamp species, collected alongside Nannostomus marginatus, N. trifasciatus, Carnegiella strigata, Laetacara fulvipinnis, and — notably — this site's own A. uaupesi. That's a genuine documented cohabitation between two species covered on this site, not an inferred one, and it's a reasonable cue that tannin-stained, soft, structurally complex blackwater conditions suit helkeri the same way they suit uaupesi.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: Fishipedia lists a 16-gallon (60L) minimum for a group of at least two — small, in line with the fish's own small adult size. See our tank setup guide for scaling up for a harem or breeding setup.
  • Substrate & décor: Not documented species-specifically, but its native blackwater-swamp habitat and co-occurrence with leaf-litter specialists like Carnegiella strigata point toward soft substrate, driftwood, and leaf litter as a safe default. A simple spawning surface such as an upturned small plant pot covers PlanetCatfish and Cichlidae.com's shared breeding-tank advice.
  • Water chemistry: Fishipedia's everyday range is pH 5.5–6.5 at 1–5 dGH; breeding-specific guidance from Cichlidae.com and PlanetCatfish nudges toward the softer end, around 5 dH. See our water parameters guide for building soft, slightly acidic water safely.
  • Filtration & flow: Not documented for this species specifically. Gentle flow and stable, mature water are the safe default used genus-wide on this site — see our water quality guide.

Feeding

Fishipedia describes helkeri as omnivorous with a carnivorous tendency, accepting dry flakes and granules, fresh food, and frozen food, with a recommendation to vary the diet and feed in small portions aimed at the bottom of the tank so this small, bottom-oriented fish can actually reach it. That lines up with Cichlidae.com and PlanetCatfish's breeding-conditioning advice of live or frozen bloodworm and brine shrimp to bring a pair into spawning condition. See our general feeding guide for broader conditioning strategy.

Temperament & Compatibility

Fishipedia rates helkeri as fairly calm and peaceful overall but territorial, and specifically flags a risk of aggression toward conspecifics and similar-looking fish — a real consideration given how closely it resembles hongsloi, since housing the two together risks both aggression and hybridization on top of the usual genus-wide rule against mixing Apistogramma species in one tank. It's diurnal and naturally pairs off rather than forming larger groups. Fishipedia rates it as not a danger to humans, for what that's worth to a home aquarist.

Breeding

Helkeri is a cave spawner, consistent with every other species on this site, laying eggs on hidden substrate that the female then guards. Fishipedia rates breeding difficulty as difficult and notes both parents guard the eggs once laid. For the breeding tank, Cichlidae.com and PlanetCatfish recommend one male with several females when no bonded pair already exists, paired with a dedicated breeding surface such as a small upturned plant pot and water nudged toward pH 6.0–6.5 and roughly 5 dH. Aquatic Republic's own species page states plainly that there is no breeding report on file for this species — a genuine documentation gap rather than a difficulty rating this guide can fully stand behind. Follow our general breeding guide for the broader conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

Helkeri is rare in the aquarium trade. Fishipedia describes it as mainly kept by experienced hobbyists, often sourced through breeding clubs rather than general retail — consistent with a species described as recently as 2013 from a specific, non-commercial stretch of Venezuela's lower Río Cuao. The practical sourcing risk here isn't just scarcity, though: because helkeri is this easily confused with the much more available hongsloi, a seller offering "helkeri" outside a specialist or club context is worth a close look at the dorsal fin and caudal fin markings described above before you buy, not just a locality claim. See our general sourcing guide for broader guidance on vetting a seller for an uncommon, easily-mislabeled species.

Final Thoughts

Helkeri isn't a species to chase for color or ease of keeping — it's a small, recently described Orinoco apisto whose whole story is a close call with a much more familiar relative. What it does offer is a genuinely useful identification lesson: if hongsloi and helkeri can overlap enough in the wild and the hobby that taxonomists needed a dedicated 2013 paper to separate them, it's worth paying attention to fin shape and markings on any apisto purchase, not just the name on the tank label. If you want the easier, better-documented version of this same lineage, start with this site's own hongsloi guide. If the genus's taxonomic edge cases are what draw you in, helkeri pairs naturally with the habitat note on uaupesi, a species it's actually been collected alongside in the wild.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Apistogramma helkeri and Apistogramma hongsloi?

Ingo Schindler and Wolfgang Staeck described helkeri in 2013 explicitly as the species "most similar to" hongsloi, and separated the two on coloration and dorsal fin shape rather than anything dramatic: helkeri has a rounded, hyaline, unmarked caudal fin and short, truncate dorsal fin lappets without extensions, plus a large vertically extended caudal spot, a chainlike or zigzag lateral band that reaches the 7th bar but stops short of the caudal fin and caudal spot, an upper pectoral spot, and three distinct abdominal stripes. On the PlanetCatfish forum, moderator Mike Wise went further, noting that helkeri resembles several hongsloi populations specifically (catalogued there as A113, A114, and A115) and that the even, non-serrated dorsal fin is the trait unique to helkeri's complex. FishBase's own common name for the species — Roundfinned dwarf cichlid — points at the same rounded-fin diagnostic. If you're offered a "hongsloi" from the lower Río Cuao or wider Orinoco drainage in Venezuela, that fin shape is worth checking before you buy.

How big does Apistogramma helkeri get?

Small even by apisto standards. Fishipedia gives an average of 1.2in (3cm) and a maximum of 1.6in (4cm); FishBase independently confirms a 4.0cm standard-length maximum for males and unsexed specimens. Neither source breaks out a separate female figure, which itself is a documentation gap worth knowing about before assuming the usual apisto pattern of a visibly larger male.

What water parameters does Apistogramma helkeri need?

Fishipedia lists 73–79°F (23–26°C), pH 5.5–6.5, and a native-habitat hardness of 1–5 dGH. Separately, Cichlidae.com and PlanetCatfish's shared breeding-tank guidance calls for slightly acidic, soft water in the same pH 6.0–6.5 and roughly 5 dH range specifically to help trigger spawning — the two sources line up well enough that this guide treats fishipedia's numbers as the safe everyday range. See our water parameters guide for building soft, slightly acidic water safely.

Is Apistogramma helkeri endangered?

The IUCN Red List assessed it as Data Deficient in 2017 — not Least Concern, not threatened, genuinely undetermined. FishBase separately rates the species' resilience as high, with a preliminary estimate of less than 15 months to double a depleted population, but that's a biological resilience estimate, not a conservation status, and it doesn't resolve the Data Deficient listing either way.

Why is Apistogramma helkeri so hard to find in the hobby?

It was only formally described in 2013, it comes from a specific stretch of Venezuela's lower Río Cuao that isn't a major collection or export hub, and fishipedia describes it as rare in the aquarium trade and mainly kept by experienced hobbyists, often sourced through breeding clubs rather than retail. Aquatic Republic's own species page states plainly that there is no breeding report on file for it — a genuine documentation gap, not just thin detail. Add in the resemblance to the much more available hongsloi, and a seller offering "helkeri" outside a specialist or club context is worth a second look at that dorsal fin.
