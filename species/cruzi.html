Apistogramma cruzi

Apistogramma cruzi

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 2" (5.1cm SL male/unsexed)
  • Tank: Not documented precisely; genus-standard 15–20g used as a working default
  • pH: 6.0+ native
  • Temperature: 79°F+ (26°C+) native; upper bound not confirmed
  • Hardness: dH 6+ native
  • Temperament: Not documented species-specifically
  • Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced — more available than most recent additions, but thin husbandry documentation

Overview

Apistogramma cruzi, the Parallel-striped dwarf cichlid, is the second species on this site that Sven Kullander himself formally described, after this site's own A. hongsloi. Kullander catalogued cruzi in 1986, from a wide swath of the western Amazon basin spanning the Napo River drainage of Peru and Ecuador and the Putumayo and Caquetá River drainages of Colombia. Between this entry, hongsloi, and this site's own A. kullanderi — the species formally named in his honor in 2014 — Kullander's work now runs through three entries in this site's library: two species he personally described decades apart, and one named for him.

The common name describes the fish directly: FishBase lists it as the "Parallel striped dwarf cichlid," for the horizontal banding along its body.

Natural Habitat

FishBase places cruzi across the Napo River drainage (Peru, Ecuador) and the Putumayo and Caquetá River drainages (Colombia), all tributaries within the wider Amazon basin. The habitat is described as slightly turbid, colorless water — not the clear-water or classic tannin-stained blackwater pictures some other apistos on this site come from.

Feeding

No source checked for this guide gives cruzi-specific feeding guidance. Follow the genus-standard approach used elsewhere on this site: small sinking foods as a daily base diet, with live or frozen bloodworm and brine shrimp for conditioning before breeding. See our general feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

No source checked for this guide rates cruzi's temperament, aggression, or tankmate compatibility in detail. Treat it like a typical mid-sized apisto until a keeper with firsthand experience documents more: calm tankmates, and caution around other Apistogramma species given the genus-wide hybridization risk.

Breeding

Cruzi is a cave spawner, consistent with every other species on this site. Sourcing Notes

Cruzi is more findable than most of this site's recent additions — UK specialist aquarium retailers have sold young stock at around 3cm — but species-specific husbandry documentation beyond basic size and native-range data is still thin. Ask any seller for the stock's regional origin within the Napo/Putumayo/Caquetá range if locality matters to your breeding or record-keeping goals. See our general sourcing guide for broader guidance on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Cruzi rounds out this site's growing record of Sven Kullander's work in this genus: he described it in 1986, described this site's own hongsloi seven years earlier in 1979, and had this site's own kullanderi named in his honor in 2014. It's a genuinely findable species with a real IUCN assessment behind it — a rarer combination on this site than you might expect — even though detailed husbandry documentation hasn't caught up yet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who described Apistogramma cruzi, and what's the connection to A. kullanderi?

Sven Kullander described cruzi in 1986. That makes it the second species in this site's library that Kullander himself formally described and catalogued, alongside this site's own A. hongsloi (1979) — and a third credited entry overall once this site's own A. kullanderi is counted, the species named in his honor in 2014. Between describing new species for decades and eventually having one named for him, Kullander's work runs through several entries in this site's library.

Why is Apistogramma cruzi called the "Parallel-striped" dwarf cichlid?

FishBase lists "Parallel striped dwarf cichlid" as its common name, referring to the horizontal striping pattern along the body that the name describes.

How big does Apistogramma cruzi get, and where is it from?

FishBase gives a maximum of 5.1cm (2in) standard length for males or unsexed fish. Its native range spans the Napo River drainage of Peru and Ecuador and the Putumayo and Caquetá River drainages of Colombia, all within the wider Amazon basin, in slightly turbid, colorless water rather than clear blackwater.

What water parameters does Apistogramma cruzi need?

FishBase lists native-habitat water as pH 6.0 and above with hardness (dH) 6 and above, and a tropical temperature of at least 26°C, though the page marks several upper bounds as unconfirmed. That's a notably less extreme range than this genus's true blackwater specialists, closer to the flexible-generalist end of the spectrum. See our water parameters guide.

Is Apistogramma cruzi available in the hobby?

More available than most of the recent additions to this site's library. UK specialist retailers have sold young cruzi at around 3cm, and FishBase lists its IUCN Red List status as Least Concern, assessed 24 April 2014 — a real conservation assessment rather than the "Not Evaluated" status several other species on this site carry.
