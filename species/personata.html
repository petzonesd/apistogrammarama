Apistogramma personata

Apistogramma personata

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 2.0" (5.2cm SL) max for males/unsexed per FishBase — no separate female figure published
  • Tank: No species-specific minimum documented; genus-standard footprint assumed
  • pH: No confirmed wild measurement found; 6.0–6.5 is a generic breeding-tank figure, not a documented requirement
  • Temperature: Not documented in any source checked for this guide
  • Hardness: ~5 dH is a generic breeding-tank figure, not a documented requirement
  • Temperament: Not documented beyond a mood-driven color/eyespot display reported in one account
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genuine trade rarity, and the main commercial form's identity is itself disputed

Overview

Apistogramma personata is a scientifically valid 1980 Kullander description that almost nobody in the hobby has actually kept under a confirmed identification. The holotype comes from the Rio Uaupés at Assaí, Amazonas State, Brazil, and FishBase lists a maximum size of 5.2cm (2.0") standard length for males or unsexed fish, with no separate female figure published and an IUCN status of Data Deficient. That's the formal science. The fish most hobbyists have actually encountered under the personata name is a different story: it was first collected in 1995 near Mitú, Colombia — a different country and river system from the holotype locality — by researcher Uwe Werner and colleagues, and the hobby simply adopted "A. personata" as its working name because that's what early literature called it.

German wholesale importer Aquarium Glaser, writing in 2017 under the deliberately hedged label "Apistogramma personata Mitu," says the question of what this fish actually is remains unresolved: Uwe Römer's own Cichliden Atlas identifies the Mitú population as Apistogramma brevis instead, and both brevis and personata are described as too poorly studied to settle the dispute. Specialist moderators on the apistogramma.com forum have added a further wrinkle, describing the true Brazilian holotype form as noticeably deeper-bodied (higher-backed) than the Colombian "cf. personata" fish typically seen in trade photos — which means even calling a given import "cf. personata" is already a hedge, not a confirmed identification.

Natural Habitat

The formally described holotype population occupies the Rio Uaupés at Assaí, in Amazonas State, Brazil — part of the Rio Negro drainage. No source checked for this guide documents specific water-chemistry readings taken at that site. The separate, far more commonly traded Colombian population associated with the personata name comes from near Mitú, on the Rio Vaupés (the Colombian name for the same river system as Brazil's Rio Uaupés downstream), documented by a 1995 collecting trip and tracked since in specialist forum threads discussing specific import shipments.

Feeding

No source checked for this guide documents a diet specific to personata. Follow the standard genus approach — foods that reach the bottom third of the tank, with live or frozen bloodworm or brine shrimp used to bring pairs into breeding condition — covered in our general feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

Documented behavior for this species is limited to one detailed account: Aquarium Glaser describes an excited male displaying distinct eyespots on its gill covers (opercles), a feature the same account says becomes hardly visible once the fish calms down. A separate forum post documents a sibling from the same Colombian import showing a fully orange face, which posters speculated might point to a relationship with this site's own gibbiceps — no source checked for this guide confirms that connection, and it's offered there as informed speculation rather than settled taxonomy. No source documents specific tankmate recommendations; treat it as an advanced, poorly-documented fish and apply the genus-standard caution against aggressive or fin-nipping tankmates.

Breeding

Documented breeding information is thin. Aquarium Glaser's account notes that live specimens Werner brought back from the original 1995 Colombian collection did not breed successfully. A separate forum thread does document one keeper's "cf. personata" group producing an estimated 60 to 70 fry from a single spawn — the only confirmed breeding success found in any source checked for this guide, though it comes with the same identification caveat attached to nearly everything about this species. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process, and don't expect a reliable track record to lean on.

Sourcing Notes

Budget for a long wait. Aquarium Glaser calls personata a "top-rarity" supplied to the wholesale trade only in small numbers, and a documented apistogramma.com forum thread shows one hobbyist celebrating finally acquiring the fish after roughly ten years of searching. If a seller offers it, ask directly whether the stock traces to the Brazilian holotype locality (Rio Uaupés at Assaí) or the far more commonly available Colombian "Mitú" population — and understand going in that even specialist forum moderators hedge the Colombian form's identification as "cf. personata" rather than confirmed. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting rarity and locality claims.

Final Thoughts

Personata is less a fish with a documented care sheet than a fish with a documented identity crisis — a formally valid 1980 species whose main hobby presence may not even be that species at all, tangled with this site's own brevis in a dispute neither side of the literature has resolved. If a confirmed identification matters to you, treat any "personata" offered for sale as "cf. personata" until proven otherwise, and budget years rather than weeks to find it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Apistogramma personata sold in the hobby actually that species?

Often uncertain. The fish most commonly traded as "A. personata" was first collected in 1995 near Mitú, Colombia, and the hobby adopted the personata name for it simply because that's what it was first called — but the formally described holotype (Kullander, 1980) comes from the Rio Uaupés at Assaí, Amazonas State, Brazil, a different country and drainage. German wholesaler Aquarium Glaser notes that researcher Uwe Römer's own Cichliden Atlas identifies the Mitú trade fish as Apistogramma brevis instead, and calls the question unresolved because both species are poorly studied. Specialist forum moderators on apistogramma.com have separately described the true holotype form as deeper-bodied (higher-backed) than the Mitú "cf. personata" fish typically seen in the trade.

How rare is Apistogramma personata in the aquarium trade?

Genuinely rare. Aquarium Glaser, a German wholesale importer, calls it a "top-rarity" among dwarf cichlids and supplies it to the wholesale trade only in small numbers. A documented apistogramma.com forum thread shows one Hong Kong-based hobbyist celebrating finally acquiring the fish after roughly ten years of waiting, with a Singapore-based keeper reporting stock from the same shipment. FishBase lists no common name and rates its IUCN status Data Deficient, and the aquarium profile database Aquatic Republic shows no registered keepers for the species at all.

Does Apistogramma personata have any notable color or display behavior?

Yes — a documented eyespot display. Aquarium Glaser's own account describes the species as not brightly colored but considerably variable in mood-driven color, and notes that an excited male shows distinct eyespots on its gill covers (opercles) that are hardly visible when the fish is calm. A separate forum post documents a sibling from the same import showing a fully orange face, which posters speculated might point to a close relationship with this site's own gibbiceps and the Mesa-Salazar & Lasso species group that includes piaroa.

How big does Apistogramma personata get, and what water does it need?

FishBase lists a maximum of 5.2cm (2.0") standard length for males or unsexed fish, with no separate female figure published. No source checked for this guide gives a confirmed water-chemistry range specific to personata; Aquatic Republic's breeding-tank recommendation of pH 6.0–6.5 and roughly 5 dH is a generic genus-level figure rather than a measurement taken from wild personata habitat, so treat it as a reasonable starting point rather than a documented requirement.
