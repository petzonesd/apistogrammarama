Apistogramma taeniata

Apistogramma taeniata

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 1.7" (42mm SL) — small, but notable for an entirely different reason
  • Tank: Not documented precisely; genus-standard 15–20g used as a working default given its small size
  • pH: 6.0–6.5 (breeding guidance)
  • Hardness: ~5°dH (breeding guidance)
  • Temperature: Not documented species-specifically; genus-standard 73–82°F used as a working default
  • Temperament: Not documented species-specifically
  • Difficulty: Advanced — historically significant, but essentially undocumented in modern aquarium trade

Overview

Apistogramma taeniata isn't on this site for its husbandry profile — it's here because every other species on this site, and every other Apistogramma on record, belongs to a genus that is formally defined around this one fish. Albert C.L.G. Günther first described it in 1862, in the Catalogue of the fishes in the British Museum, under the name Mesops taeniatus. At that point, the genus Apistogramma simply didn't exist yet. It took until 1913 for Charles Tate Regan, working on a collection of fishes from Peru's Río Ucayali, to erect the new genus Apistogramma — and when he did, he formally designated Günther's half-century-old Mesops taeniatus as its type species.

A type species is the taxonomic anchor point for a genus: the single species a genus's formal definition is built around, used to settle disputes about what does or doesn't belong in it. Every species on this site — cacatuoides, kullanderi, all the rest — is classified into Apistogramma because it shares the defining traits Regan drew from this fish. It's a piece of pure taxonomic history, not a popular aquarium species, but it belongs on a site built around understanding this genus properly.

Natural Habitat

Taeniata's type locality is the Rio Cupari (also spelled Rio Cupai), part of the lower Río Tapajós basin in Pará state, Brazil — within the wider Amazon drainage. No source checked for this guide documents additional range beyond this type locality region.

Feeding

Documented guidance matches the genus-standard pattern used throughout this site: mainly sinking foods that reach the bottom third of the tank, such as flakes and granules, with live or frozen bloodworm or brine shrimp recommended to bring a pair into breeding condition. See our general feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

No source checked for this guide rates taeniata's temperament or tankmate compatibility in any detail — a reflection of how little modern aquarium-keeping documentation exists for this historically important but rarely-traded species. Treat it like a typical small apisto until a keeper with firsthand experience documents more.

Breeding

Taeniata is a cave spawner, consistent with every other species on this site. Documented breeding-tank guidance recommends one male with several females when no bonded pair already exists, paired with a small upturned plant pot or similar spawning surface and water nudged to pH 6.0–6.5 at roughly 5°dH. No detailed fry-care timeline specific to this species was found in the sources checked for this guide. Follow our general breeding guide for the broader conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

Taeniata isn't a species you're likely to find by shopping — no source checked for this guide documents any regular aquarium trade for it, and it doesn't appear in SeriouslyFish's individual-species listing for this genus. If you do encounter a fish sold under this name, its real value to this genus is historical rather than commercial, so prioritize confirming the taxonomic identity (ideally through a specialist who can speak to its Tapajós-basin origin) over any claim about rarity or collector value. See our general sourcing guide for broader guidance on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Taeniata will never be a beginner's first apisto, and it probably won't be anyone's tenth, either — but it's the fish the entire genus Apistogramma is taxonomically anchored to, described half a century before that genus existed and only formally designated its type species in 1913. Every other species on this site inherits its genus membership from the definition Regan built around this one. That's a different kind of significance than a care-difficulty rating captures, and it's worth knowing regardless of whether you ever keep the fish itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean that Apistogramma taeniata is the genus's "type species"?

It means this specific species is the scientific reference point the entire genus Apistogramma is formally defined against. Albert C.L.G. Günther first described the fish in 1862, in the Catalogue of the fishes in the British Museum, under the name Mesops taeniatus — the genus Apistogramma didn't exist yet. Charles Tate Regan erected Apistogramma in 1913, in a paper on fishes from Peru's Río Ucayali, and formally designated Mesops taeniatus Günther, 1862 as that new genus's type species. In practical terms, every other Apistogramma species on this site — all 49 others — is classified into a genus that is, taxonomically speaking, built around this one fish.

How big does Apistogramma taeniata get, and where is it from?

Small. Aquatic Republic gives a maximum of 42mm (1.7in) standard length. Its native range and type locality is the Rio Cupari (Rio Cupai), part of the lower Río Tapajós basin in Pará state, Brazil, within the wider Amazon drainage.

What water parameters does Apistogramma taeniata need?

Aquatic Republic's breeding-tank guidance calls for slightly acidic, soft water: pH 6.0–6.5 and roughly 5°dH. No separate general-maintenance range distinct from the breeding figures was found in the sources checked for this guide. See our water parameters guide.

Is Apistogramma taeniata endangered?

No. Its IUCN Red List status is Least Concern, last assessed in 2018.

Is Apistogramma taeniata easy to find in the hobby?

No — despite its taxonomic importance, no source checked for this guide documents regular trade availability, and it isn't covered by SeriouslyFish's individual-species listing for this genus. Its significance is historical and scientific rather than a reflection of how commonly it's kept.
