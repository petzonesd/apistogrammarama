Apistogramma trifasciata

Three-Striped Dwarf Cichlid

By Pet Zone SD Team • October 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Male to 2.4" (6 cm), female to 1.6" (4 cm)
  • Tank: 15–20 gallons for a pair; nano-friendly for its size
  • pH: 5.5–7.5 — one of the more adaptable apistos
  • Temperature: 72–82°F (22–28°C)
  • Hardness: Soft, up to 12 dGH
  • Temperament: Peaceful, harem-compatible
  • Difficulty: Beginner

Overview

Apistogramma trifasciata, the three-striped dwarf cichlid, is one of the smallest and most adaptable apistos in the hobby, sharing borellii's tolerance for cooler water and small tanks while shrugging off a wider pH swing than most of the genus. It ranges across the Paraguay-Paraná and Guaporé basins of Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina — often turning up in the same waters as borellii, which makes the two frequent nano-tank rivals in the hobby. Several regional color forms circulate under names like "blue," "red," and "Paraguay," each with slightly different fin coloration, but all share the three dark horizontal body stripes that give the species its name.

Males show a blue-green iridescent sheen across the flanks with yellow-to-orange unpaired fins edged in blue or black, while females are smaller, rounder, and flash a bright yellow when guarding fry — a family resemblance to borellii that isn't a coincidence given how closely their ranges overlap.

Tank Setup

A pair or small harem does well in a 15–20 gallon tank. Like borellii, trifasciata runs small and calm enough for a genuinely nano setup — a heavily planted 10–15 gallon tank can house a pair if the aquascape breaks up sightlines into separate territories.

  • Substrate: Fine sand. Trifasciata forages in the substrate throughout the day.
  • Leaf litter: Indian almond or oak leaves mimic the slow blackwater and whitewater creek margins this species favors, and help buffer pH downward.
  • Hardscape: Driftwood and dense planting with multiple caves — one per female when keeping a harem, so each can claim her own territory.
  • Filtration: Gentle flow. This species comes from slow-moving, often vegetated water, not current.
  • Lighting: Moderate to dim, with floating plants to soften the surface and encourage natural color.

Feeding

An omnivore that picks at small insects, crustaceans, and detritus in the wild. In the tank, a quality flake or micro-pellet base supplemented with live or frozen baby brine shrimp, daphnia, and microworms will keep color sharp and condition fish for breeding. Feed small amounts twice daily — this species' small mouth means food size matters more than with larger apistos.

Temperament & Compatibility

Trifasciata sits at the peaceful end of the genus, similar to borellii. A harem of one male to two or three females works in a tank with enough visual breaks, since each female holds her own small territory and the male rotates attention between them. Good tankmates include small tetras, corydoras, and other peaceful bottom and mid-water fish that won't compete hard for food.

Avoid housing with other Apistogramma species in anything but a large, heavily broken-up tank — hybridization between closely related species, including borellii, is a real risk if territories overlap during spawning.

Breeding

Conditioning

Condition the group on live and frozen foods for two to three weeks before expecting spawning activity. Trifasciata is one of the more reliable apistos to breed once conditions are right, putting it alongside cacatuoides and borellii as a good early breeding project.

Spawning

A cave spawner. The female lays eggs inside a cave or similar enclosed space and takes over nearly all brood care, chasing off anything that approaches — including, at times, the male — while he patrols the wider territory.

Fry Care

Eggs typically hatch within 2–3 days, with fry free-swimming about a week later. Start fry on infusoria or a liquid fry food for the first several days before moving to newly hatched brine shrimp and microworms. Because the fry are small even by apisto standards, keep food particle size small and water quality stable — this species is no more forgiving of ammonia or nitrite spikes during the fry stage than the rest of the genus.

Where to Get Quality Fish

Look for fish that are alert and curious at the front of the tank, with clean fins and even coloration — trifasciata that are stressed or poorly acclimated tend to hide and wash out in color. Because several regional color forms are sold under informal trade names, ask the seller which locality or strain a fish traces to if you want to keep a line pure rather than mix forms. Final Thoughts

If you like borellii for its calm temperament and nano-tank manners, trifasciata is the natural next species to try — smaller still in the female, just as tolerant of a planted nano setup, and arguably even more forgiving of water-chemistry drift. It's an excellent choice for a second or third apisto, or a first one for a keeper who already runs a small, well-planted tank.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Apistogramma trifasciata good for beginners?

Yes. Trifasciata is one of the most adaptable apistos in the hobby, tolerating a wider pH and temperature range than most of the genus, which makes it forgiving of the small water-chemistry mistakes beginners tend to make.

What is the difference between trifasciata and borellii?

They share range and size, but trifasciata runs a touch smaller, especially in females, tolerates a broader pH swing, and shows its signature three dark body stripes rather than borellii's solid umbrella-fin display. Either works well in a nano planted tank.

Can trifasciata live in a nano tank?

Yes. A single pair or a small harem can thrive in a heavily planted 10 to 15 gallon tank, making it one of the few apistos genuinely suited to nano setups.

How many females per male for trifasciata?

A harem of one male to two or three females works well in a tank with enough line-of-sight breaks, since females will hold separate territories and the male divides his attention between them.

Is trifasciata easy to breed?

Yes, it is one of the more reliable apistos to spawn once a pair is conditioned, ranking alongside cacatuoides and borellii as a good early breeding project.
