Apistogramma wolli

Apistogramma wolli

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: FishBase gives two conflicting figures — 2.0" (5.1cm SL) in its summary field, or 2.4"/1.6" (62mm/40mm SL) male/female in its morphology section
  • Tank: Not documented; genus-standard 15–20g default by body size
  • pH: 6.3+ per FishBase (no upper bound published); one hobbyist reports breeding success at pH 5.1
  • Temperature: 77°F+ (25°C+) per available sources; no upper bound published
  • Hardness: Not formally documented; hobbyist anecdotes cite low TDS (~54 ppm) and EC ~30
  • Temperament: Conflicting hobbyist reports — one group described as passive and repeatedly bred, a separate specimen described as showing "extreme aggression"
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genuine trade rarity, wild-caught sourcing only, thinly documented

Overview

Apistogramma wolli carries a hobbyist's name and a hobbyist's confusion in equal measure. Uwe Römer and five co-authors formally described it in 2015, honoring Wolfgang "Wolli" Friedrich, a German professional fish breeder who had closed his own breeding facility only shortly before. The same paper did double taxonomic duty: it re-described A. payaminonis (originally named by Kullander in 1986) and named a second brand-new species, A. feconat, from the same Peru/Ecuador border-region survey — three species' worth of work from one publication. That shared origin has made its way into the trade as genuine confusion rather than just a footnote: a hobbyist forum thread notes dealers listing wild-caught fish as "payaminonis" when they were actually either wolli or feconat, depending on exactly where they were collected.

FishBase's morphology notes describe adult males with massive, hypertrophied yellow jaws and lips, serrated dorsal fin membranes, and a lyrate (deeply forked) caudal fin carrying a red marginal band — the likely source of the "Bloodfinned" common name. Females have a rounded caudal fin instead. On a 2024 identification thread, moderator Mike Wise drew a separate, more technical distinction from a superficially similar undescribed form known only as "sp. D3": D3 females show two lateral spots in brood dress, while wolli females show just one. Both the dealer mix-up and the D3 look-alike problem are exactly the kind of identification pitfall this site tracks across the genus.

Natural Habitat

Aquatic Republic places the type locality in Peru's Loreto Province, Maynas District, near Cabo Pontoja — a small forest stream feeding the Río Napo close to the Ecuador border, at roughly 75°12′W, 0°57′S. FishBase's broader habitat description extends that picture to small, shallow blackwater rainforest streams running along both sides of the Ecuador–Peru border, where the fish shelter among leaf litter over sandy bottoms with only scarce driftwood for cover. Worth noting: FishBase's own distribution field lists only Peru, even though its habitat text describes streams on both sides of the border — a minor internal inconsistency on FishBase's part, not this guide's.

Tank Setup

No source checked for this guide documents a tested aquarium setup beyond the scattered breeding-tank notes below — wolli is simply too rare in the hobby for that kind of detail to exist yet.

  • Tank size: Not documented. One keeper on a hobbyist forum kept a wild-caught breeding pair in a 10-gallon tank; given the species' small adult size either way, a 15–20 gallon setup is a reasonable genus-standard starting point. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: Not documented species-specifically, but its blackwater, leaf-litter-and-sand habitat points toward a soft substrate with leaf litter and driftwood rather than an open, rocky layout.
  • Water chemistry: FishBase gives only a lower-bound pH of 6.3 from wild habitat data; a hobbyist keeping a breeding pair reports success at a notably lower, more acidic pH 5.1 with low TDS (54 ppm). See our water parameters guide for building soft, acidic water safely.
  • Filtration & flow: Not documented. A gentle-flow default is reasonable given the species' still, shallow forest-stream origin.

Feeding

Aquatic Republic's genus-standard guidance applies: offer food that reaches the bottom third of the tank, with sinking flakes and granules as a daily base. For conditioning a breeding pair specifically, it recommends live or frozen bloodworm and brine shrimp. No source checked for this guide documents anything more species-specific. See our general feeding guide for broader strategy.

Temperament & Compatibility

Documentation here genuinely conflicts rather than simply being thin. On the same December 2024 forum thread, one keeper described their wolli group's behavior as passive even while breeding repeatedly, while a second keeper reported a wolli in their own tank showing "extreme aggression." Neither account gives enough context (group size, tank size, tankmates) to say which is more typical. Treat this as a real documented inconsistency and keep a close eye on a newly introduced wolli's behavior yourself rather than assuming either extreme. As with every species on this site, never house multiple Apistogramma species together given the hybridization risk.

Breeding

Wolli is further along than its database listings suggest. Aquatic Republic's own species page — typically this site's first check for a registered breeding report — still lists breeding as "unreported." Real hobbyist accounts tell a different story: a December 2024 apistogramma.com forum thread documents a wild-caught pair producing fry on two separate occasions, with the keeper describing the process as nothing out of the ordinary once water parameters were easy to maintain. A second keeper in the same thread reports roughly four successful spawns from a separate group. Neither account gives egg counts, incubation timing, or fry-rearing detail beyond the fact of successful spawning — so while the fact of captive breeding is well documented, the mechanics aren't yet. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process until more detail is published.

Sourcing Notes

Every source checked for this guide describes wolli as wild-caught stock rather than a captive-bred farm fish — the same forum thread documenting its breeding is literally titled "WC Apistogramma wolli with fry," underlining how new any captive line still is. The dealer confusion with payaminonis and feconat, both described in the same 2015 paper from the same border region, is the single biggest sourcing risk: ask any seller specifically which of the three species a "payaminonis"-labeled fish actually is, and where exactly it was collected, since the paper's own authors used collection locality to separate the three. If you're offered a fish as the look-alike undescribed form "sp. D3" instead, know that hobbyist moderators distinguish it from true wolli by the female's lateral-spot count in brood dress — a detail worth asking an experienced seller about directly. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's identification claims. Separately, German importer/publisher Aqualog has captioned wild-import photos "Apistogramma atahualpa (= sp. aff. payaminonis)" — a distinct, Aqualog-documented note tying this site's own A. atahualpa to the same payaminonis complex, worth knowing if you're untangling labels across more than one dealer.

Final Thoughts

Wolli is a genuine rarity with an unusually tangled paper trail: one 2015 publication, three species, and a trade that still mixes at least two of them up. If its scarcity and documentation gaps give you pause, this site's own cacatuoides offers a far better-documented, far more available entry point into the genus. If the appeal is precisely in chasing down a species this thinly recorded — and in sorting real identification from dealer shorthand — wolli is as current an example as this site's species library gets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Apistogramma wolli named after?

Wolfgang "Wolli" Friedrich, a German professional fish breeder who closed his breeding facility in early 2014, shortly before the species was formally described. Römer, Soares, Dávila, Duponchelle, Renno and Hahn published the description in 2015 in the same paper that re-described A. payaminonis and named a second new species, A. feconat — three species' worth of taxonomy from one Peru/Ecuador border-region survey.

How is Apistogramma wolli distinguished from similar species?

By a combination of features: hypertrophied, massive yellow jaws and lips on adult males, serrated dorsal fin membranes, a lyrate (deeply forked) caudal fin on males with a red marginal band versus a rounded caudal fin on females, and a large round caudal spot. On a hobbyist identification forum, moderator Mike Wise separately distinguished it from the superficially similar undescribed form "sp. D3": D3 females show two lateral spots in brood dress, while wolli females show only one. Dealers have also been reported listing fish as "payaminonis" when they were actually either wolli or feconat, depending on the collection locality — exactly the kind of trade-name confusion this site flags across the genus.

What water parameters does Apistogramma wolli need?

Documentation is thin and conflicting. FishBase's wild-habitat data gives only a lower bound: pH 6.3 and up, at 25°C and up, with no published upper limits. A hobbyist forum thread describing a wild-caught breeding pair reports a notably more acidic captive setup — pH 5.1 at low TDS (54 ppm) in one keeper's 10-gallon tank, and roughly 77°F in another's softer-water setup. Treat the FishBase figures as the only peer-reviewed data point and the forum numbers as real but anecdotal captive-keeping reports, not a confirmed general range.

Has Apistogramma wolli been bred in captivity?

Yes, by hobbyists, even though the formal trade-reference databases haven't caught up. Aquatic Republic's species page still lists breeding as "unreported," but a December 2024 apistogramma.com forum thread documents a wild-caught pair producing fry on two separate occasions, and a second keeper in the same thread reports roughly four successful spawns from their own group. The gap between the official "no breeding report" listing and these real hobbyist accounts is itself worth knowing if you're researching this species — don't mistake database silence for genuine difficulty.

How big does Apistogramma wolli get?

FishBase gives two figures that don't agree: its summary field lists a maximum of 5.1cm (2.0in) standard length for males/unsexed fish, while the same page's morphology section separately states males reach 62mm (2.4in) SL and females 40mm (1.6in) SL. No source checked for this guide reconciles the discrepancy, so both figures are reported here rather than silently picking one.
