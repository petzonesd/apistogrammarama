Apistogramma juruensis

Apistogramma juruensis

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 0.9" (2.4cm SL male/unsexed) — one of the smallest species on this site
  • Tank: Not documented; genus-standard 10–15g used as a working default given its small size
  • pH: Not documented from wild habitat; genus-standard 5.5–7.0 used as a working default
  • Temperature: 72–84°F (22–29°C), per native-habitat data
  • Hardness: Not documented from wild habitat
  • Temperament: Not documented species-specifically
  • Difficulty: Advanced — thin documentation and almost no trade history under its correct name

Overview

Apistogramma juruensis is a genuinely obscure species in the hobby — but it has an outsized role in this genus's trade history, as the name a completely different, more commonly-kept fish was sold under for nearly two decades. Sven Kullander formally described juruensis in 1986 from Brazil's upper Río Juruá, a tributary deep in the Amazon basin. That would likely have been the end of its story for most keepers, except that a Peruvian species — this site's own A. allpahuayo — was imported and sold under the name "Apistogramma juruensis" from at least 1995 onward, for a run of roughly 17 years, before a 2012 formal description finally separated the two and gave allpahuayo its own name.

The two species aren't close look-alikes resolved by a subtle fin-count difference — they're genuinely different fish from two different countries, and the mix-up appears to have been a trade-name convenience rather than a taxonomic dispute. If you're shopping for either species today, this is exactly the kind of history worth knowing: a trade name can outlive its accuracy by decades.

Natural Habitat

FishBase places juruensis in the upper Río Juruá basin of Brazil, within the wider Amazon drainage, and names that system as the species' type locality. Native habitat data describes freshwater, benthopelagic conditions at a tropical 22–29°C, with females tending eggs laid on cave ceilings — the standard genus-wide pattern. No pH or hardness figures from the wild habitat were published in the sources checked for this guide, which is typical for a species with this little aquarium-trade documentation under its own name.

Given its very small size, treat it with the same caution this site recommends for other diminutive species: calm, equally small tankmates, and no larger or more boisterous fish that could outcompete it for food.

Sourcing Notes

Juruensis is a genuine lesson in why a trade name alone isn't proof of identity. For at least 17 years, fish sold as "Apistogramma juruensis" in the aquarium trade were very often this site's own A. allpahuayo — a different species from a different country — until a 2012 scientific description finally separated the two. If you're offered a fish specifically labeled juruensis today, ask the seller whether the identification has been checked against Kullander's 1986 description and its confirmed Brazilian Río Juruá origin, rather than inherited from older trade literature that may still be conflating it with allpahuayo. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Juruensis is a hard species to recommend on care merits alone — it's small, thinly documented, and rarely available under its correct identity. What it offers instead is a genuinely useful piece of context for anyone shopping this genus: it's the real fish whose name this site's own allpahuayo borrowed, unintentionally, for nearly two decades. Knowing both species exist — and that a shared trade name doesn't mean a shared identity — is good practice for this entire genus, not just these two fish.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Apistogramma juruensis matter to this site specifically?

Because it's the real species behind one of this genus's clearest trade misidentification cases. This site's own A. allpahuayo was imported and sold under the name "Apistogramma juruensis" for at least 17 years, from around 1995 until a 2012 formal description gave allpahuayo its own identity. Juruensis itself is a real, distinct species, formally described by Sven Kullander in 1986 from Brazil's upper Río Juruá — a different river system from allpahuayo's Peruvian range near Iquitos. The mix-up was a trade-name problem, not a scientific dispute: two genuinely different fish from two different countries, sold for years under one name.

How big does Apistogramma juruensis get?

Small. FishBase gives a maximum of 2.4cm (0.9in) standard length for males or unsexed fish, citing the original description — one of the smaller species on this site, in the same size range as this site's own angayuara and salpinction.

Where does Apistogramma juruensis actually come from?

FishBase lists its native range as the upper Río Juruá basin in Brazil, within the wider Amazon drainage — its type locality. That's a genuinely different country and river system from allpahuayo's confirmed Peruvian range near Iquitos, which is part of why the 17-year trade mix-up between the two is considered a straightforward misidentification rather than a case of disputed taxonomy.

What water parameters does Apistogramma juruensis need?

FishBase documents its native habitat as tropical freshwater at 22–29°C, but does not publish pH or hardness figures from the wild habitat. No aquarium-specific source checked for this guide fills that gap, which is typical for a species this small and this rarely kept under its correct name. Treat genus-standard soft, slightly acidic water as the working default. See our water parameters guide.

Is Apistogramma juruensis endangered?

No. FishBase lists its IUCN Red List status as Least Concern, assessed 7 November 2018.
