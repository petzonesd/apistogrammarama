Apistogramma flabellicauda

Apistogramma flabellicauda

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: Around 7cm (2.75") per most hobby sources — one species database figure of 8.5" SL is almost certainly a data error and shouldn't be trusted
  • Tank: Documented successful breeding in a 100x40x45cm (roughly 40x16x18") tank
  • pH: ~4.5 per wholesale-exporter habitat data; a documented breeding account ran pH 4.2
  • Temperature: 24–28°C (75–82°F) — lower end for care, upper end for breeding; documented breeding ran 25–26°C (77–79°F)
  • Hardness: Barely measurable in the wild; a documented breeding account ran ~50 µS/cm conductivity, effectively zero
  • Temperament: Not specifically documented by primary sources checked for this guide — treat as standard genus caution until proven otherwise
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genus-extreme water chemistry, genuinely disputed taxonomic validity, and (per this guide's own research) a harder-won breeding record than its species database entry suggests

Overview

Apistogramma flabellicauda is one of eight Apistogramma species described in a single 2011 revision of the genus across the Orinoco basin, and German wholesale exporter Aquarium Glaser argues it may not hold up as a species distinct from this site's own A. uaupesi at all. Lina Mesa Salazar and Carlos Lasso published the description as part of "Revisión del género Apistogramma Regan, 1913 (Perciformes, Cichlidae) en la cuenca del río Orinoco," a 192-page monograph for Colombia's Instituto Alexander von Humboldt that named flabellicauda alongside seven other new species in one release — caudomaculata, intermedia, lineata, minima, nororientalis, pedunculata, and piaroa. The name itself describes the fish rather than honoring a person: flabellicauda combines Latin flabellum, a large ceremonial fan, with cauda, tail, for the fan-shaped spread of pigmented crossbars across the caudal fin.

Aquarium Glaser's own fish-archive entry is unusually blunt about the taxonomy: it groups flabellicauda with uaupesi and a third 2011 description, A. lineata, as "three species of Apistogramma that even a very experienced Apistogramma enthusiast cannot reliably distinguish from one another," noting that all three were separated mainly on color characteristics that "have not proven to be consistent in breeding stock." The source goes on to argue that, for breeding purposes, it barely matters which name is used — what matters is keeping a given locality's bloodline pure rather than crossing color forms into an aquarium-only hybrid. This guide follows that same logic: flabellicauda is treated here as its own documented entity because that's the name under which the husbandry and breeding data below were recorded, not because its taxonomic validity is settled.

Appearance & Identification

The defining visual trait, per the original description's own etymology, is the caudal fin: a fan-like spread of transverse pigmented bands across the tail, broader and more elaborate than the plainer or differently patterned tails of close relatives. Species database Aquatic Republic also notes a silvery body with three longitudinal abdominal stripes, and that the lateral band extends out onto the middle of the caudal fin — consistent with, and probably the basis for, the "fan-tail" name. As with the rest of the genus, expect males to run larger and more vividly patterned than females, which stay plainer outside of breeding condition.

One figure to actively distrust: Aquatic Republic's own data sheet lists a maximum size of 215mm (8.5") SL for this species — roughly the size of a large angelfish, not a dwarf cichlid, and wildly out of step with every other source checked, which put it around 7cm (2.75"). This is almost certainly a database entry error, not a real outlier measurement, and shouldn't be used for stocking or tank-size planning.

Natural Habitat

Sources genuinely disagree on which country this species comes from. Aquatic Republic, citing the original 2011 description directly, gives a precise type locality: the Río Autana near the community of La Ceguera, Amazonas state, Venezuela, at coordinates 4°48'08.6"N, 67°29'10.2"W. Aquarium Glaser's practical husbandry page instead places the species' range in the upper Orinoco basin "in Colombia." The upper Orinoco does border both countries, so this may be an imprecise secondhand description on Aquarium Glaser's part rather than a genuine two-population range dispute — but no source checked for this guide resolves the discrepancy, so it's presented here rather than silently picked for the reader.

Both sources agree on the habitat type: slow-moving to near-stagnant blackwater over fine sand, structured by submerged branches and accumulated leaf litter rather than rock or dense planting, with the characteristic tea-brown stain of tannins leaching from decaying wood and leaves. Aquarium Glaser gives a specific pH of around 4.5 with hardness "hardly detectable."

Tank Setup

This is genus-extreme blackwater chemistry, and the documented breeding account below closely tracks the wild-habitat numbers rather than drifting toward easier parameters in captivity.

  • Tank size: The one documented breeding account checked for this guide used a 100x40x45cm tank (roughly 40x16x18", about 45 gallons) — treat that as a demonstrated successful spawning-tank size rather than an established minimum, since no source gives a separate general-keeping figure. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate: Fine sand is specifically called out by Aquarium Glaser as important for this species group — the fish root in it to feed, and coarse substrate risks mouth and gill injury that can lead to infection.
  • Hardscape & décor: Driftwood and dead leaf litter rather than rockwork, replicating the branch-and-leaf-litter structure of the wild habitat; this also helps maintain the tannin-stained water chemistry the species needs.
  • Water chemistry: Aim for pH around 4.5 for general care, tightening toward the documented breeding account's pH 4.2 and near-zero hardness (~50 µS/cm conductivity) if you intend to spawn the fish. See our water parameters guide for how to reach and hold chemistry this extreme.
  • Temperature: 24–28°C (75–82°F) per Aquarium Glaser, lower end for everyday care and upper end for breeding; the documented spawn ran 25–26°C (77–79°F), within that range.
  • Diet caution: Aquarium Glaser specifically warns against feeding Tubifex or red mosquito larvae (bloodworm) sourced from organically polluted waters, though it considers well-rinsed, low-bacteria stock of either safe. See our feeding guide for genus-standard diet practice.

Temperament & Compatibility

No primary source checked for this guide documents this species' temperament in specific detail — an unusual gap given how much husbandry data Aquarium Glaser provides on water chemistry and diet. Treat it with the same standard genus caution as any Apistogramma until a more detailed account surfaces: avoid mixed-species Apistogramma tanks given the well-documented cross-spawning and hybridization risk covered on other species pages (see this site's own tucurui page for a specific example), and provide broken sightlines and enough territory for a guarding female.

Breeding

Species database Aquatic Republic states flatly, "There is no breeding report" for this species — but a documented apistogramma.com forum account directly contradicts that, and the real story is more interesting than either a clean "yes" or "no." A hobbyist posting as Jacobus described a pair that failed four consecutive spawns: each time, the fry were deposited outside the cave in the sand roughly a week after spawning, and despite the female continuing to guard the dwindling brood, every fry died within three to four days without ever properly starting to swim. Rather than repeat a losing pattern, Jacobus separated the female from the male for several weeks to rest and recondition her on Daphnia, white mosquito larvae, and grown Artemia before reuniting the pair. Whether that specific intervention was the fix is left openly uncertain in the original account — but the fifth spawn produced fry with real vitality and motility, in a good number. The documented tank was 100x40x45cm at pH 4.2, roughly 50 µS/cm conductivity, and 25–26°C (77–79°F).

A separate apistogramma.com forum thread from a different hobbyist asks specifically how flabellicauda and A. elizabethae compare in breeding difficulty to Taenicara candidi, suggesting genuine, ongoing hobbyist interest in sourcing and spawning this species rather than it being a forgotten rarity. See our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process this genus follows, and note that this species' documented record argues for patience through early failed spawns rather than assuming something is wrong with the pair.

Sourcing Notes

This isn't a species with an established retail presence. Aquarium Glaser's practical husbandry page is itself written from a wholesale perspective and states plainly that it sells only to trade customers, not individual hobbyists — consistent with how most of this genus's rarer Orinoco species reach keepers at all: through a specialist importer or breeder rather than a general fish store. The two documented forum accounts used for this guide both describe hobbyists already holding breeding stock rather than describing a recent retail purchase, so expect to source this species through a dedicated Apistogramma club, forum, or specialist breeder rather than off a standard stock list. See our general sourcing guide for vetting a seller on an uncommon species, and given the taxonomic uncertainty discussed above, ask for collection-locality data with any specimens you do find — that's the detail genuinely worth preserving, more than the name on the label.

Final Thoughts

Flabellicauda sits in an unusual spot: genuinely rare in the trade, genuinely disputed as a taxonomically distinct species from uaupesi and lineata, and — per this guide's own research — genuinely better-documented for breeding than its own species database entry admits. If extreme soft-acid blackwater chemistry and a fish whose very validity is an open scientific question appeal to you, this is a worthwhile target; if you want the same look with a more settled taxonomic story, this site's own A. uaupesi page covers the most likely close relative, described three decades earlier from a different river system entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Apistogramma flabellicauda mean, and is it really a separate species?

Flabellicauda combines Latin flabellum (a ceremonial fan) and cauda (tail), describing the fan-shaped pattern of pigmented crossbars on the caudal fin. Mesa Salazar & Lasso formally described it in 2011 as part of a single Orinoco-wide revision that named eight new Apistogramma species at once. German wholesale exporter Aquarium Glaser argues its validity is genuinely shaky: it, A. uaupesi, and A. lineata were separated mainly by color pattern, which the same source says doesn't stay consistent within a single breeding brood — so whether this is truly distinct from uaupesi remains an open question this guide doesn't resolve.

Is Apistogramma flabellicauda easy to breed?

Sources disagree sharply on whether it's even documented at all. The species database Aquatic Republic states flatly, "There is no breeding report." But a documented apistogramma.com forum account from a hobbyist using the screen name Jacobus describes a pair that failed four spawns in a row — fry kept dying in the sand outside the cave — before a fifth spawn, following a rest period where the female was separated and fattened up, produced a good number of viable, motile fry. This guide treats it as genuinely documented, not unknown, while flagging that it took real trial and error.

Where does Apistogramma flabellicauda actually come from?

Sources disagree. The original description (per species database Aquatic Republic, citing the 2011 paper directly) gives a precise type locality in Venezuela: the Río Autana near the community of La Ceguera, Amazonas state. German wholesale exporter Aquarium Glaser instead describes the species' range as the upper Orinoco basin "in Colombia." Both countries do border the upper Orinoco, so this may simply be an imprecise secondhand description rather than a real range dispute — but no source checked for this guide resolves it outright.

What water parameters does Apistogramma flabellicauda need?

Extreme soft-acid blackwater. Aquarium Glaser describes the wild habitat as clear water over fine white sand with a pH around 4.5 and barely measurable hardness, at 24–28°C (75–82°F) — the lower end for general care, the upper end for breeding. The one documented breeding account on record used pH 4.2, conductivity around 50 µS/cm (effectively zero hardness), and 25–26°C (77–79°F) in a 100x40x45cm tank, closely matching Aquarium Glaser's own figures.
