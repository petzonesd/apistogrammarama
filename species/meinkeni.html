Apistogramma meinkeni

Apistogramma meinkeni

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 3.5cm (1.4") SL male/unsexed, 4.5cm (1.8") SL female — FishBase; a separate note gives 6.0cm TL, unreconciled (see FAQ)
  • Tank: standard apisto footprint; no species-specific minimum documented, 20-gallon used in a successful documented spawn
  • pH: 5.0–6.0 general; 5.8 in a documented successful spawn
  • Temperature: 72–84°F (22–29°C); 80°F (27°C) in a documented spawn
  • Hardness: 6–10 dH general; soft rainwater in a documented spawn
  • Temperament: Cave spawner; kept in pairs, tolerated mixed-community tetras in a documented account
  • Difficulty: Reported as "difficult" in older references, but not borne out in a documented hobbyist breeding; a real trade mislabeling risk complicates sourcing

Overview

Apistogramma meinkeni is an Amazon-basin dwarf cichlid from Brazil's Rio Uaupés, named for one of the aquarium hobby's own founding figures rather than a collector or taxonomist. Sven O. Kullander formally described it in 1980 (Bonner Zoologische Monographien No. 14, p. 118, Fig. 15), from a holotype (IRSNB 567) and 34 paratypes (IRSNB 568 and 569) held at the Royal Belgian Institute of Natural Sciences in Brussels. The type locality is a small tributary of the Rio Uaupés at a site called "Trovão," roughly 20km from where the Uaupés meets the Rio Negro, in Amazonas State, Brazil.

The species honors Hermann Meinken (1896–1976), a German high-school biology teacher who became one of the aquarium hobby's most prolific amateur ichthyologists. Meinken headed the V.D.A.'s fish identification office from 1949 until his death, co-founded a commission to standardize German common names for ornamental fish in 1951, and personally described 57 new species between 1929 and 1975 — 24 of which, including the well-known Trigonostigma hengeli, remain valid today. He co-authored the loose-leaf reference work known to generations of German aquarists simply as "Holly–Meinken–Rachow." In 1973, the University of Bremen awarded him an honorary doctorate despite his never completing a formal university degree, accepting his roughly 800 publications in place of a dissertation — a genuine "built the hobby's reference literature from the ground up" story behind the name.

This site's own A. uaupesi page already covers a second species from the same general Rio Uaupés/upper-Rio-Negro region, and notes that at least one major European wholesaler, Aquarium Glaser, trades that species strictly wholesale rather than to individual hobbyists — the same wholesaler turns up again below in meinkeni's own documented mislabeling problem.

Natural Habitat

FishBase places meinkeni's range in the Rio Uaupés drainage of the Amazon basin, a Rio Negro tributary that forms part of the Brazil–Colombia border region. The species is benthopelagic in tropical freshwater, with FishBase citing a temperature range of 22–29°C (72–84°F), pH 5.0–6.0, and general hardness of 6–10 dH. The IUCN Red List assessed the species on 7 November 2018 and rates it Least Concern, with high resilience (population doubling time under 15 months) and low vulnerability to fishing pressure — a comparatively low-stakes conservation picture next to several rarer, more range-restricted species on this site.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: No source checked for this guide gives a species-specific minimum footprint. A documented successful spawn used a single 20-gallon tank housing two pairs plus community tetras, which is a reasonable starting reference for a small group, though not a formal minimum. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate & décor: Wood and stone decor with a cave or cave-like structure for spawning — the documented breeding account used the underside of a clay pot specifically. Genus-standard dense planting and leaf litter or similar cover suit this species' soft, acidic native water.
  • Water chemistry: FishBase's general range is pH 5.0–6.0 with 6–10 dH; the documented successful spawn used softer rainwater at pH 5.8, within that range but toward its acidic end. See our water parameters guide.
  • Water quality: The documented breeding account used 25% water changes three times weekly during fry-rearing — a frequent, moderate-volume schedule worth following during breeding attempts. See our water quality guide.

Feeding

The documented hobbyist account describes meinkeni taking live, frozen, and dry foods without being finicky, feeding dry food at least once daily, and even picking at algae growing on wood and stone decor — a genus-typical, unfussy diet. See our general feeding guide for broader conditioning advice ahead of breeding.

Temperament & Compatibility

The one documented account available describes meinkeni cohabiting successfully with a few tetras in the same tank, consistent with the genus's usual peaceful-outside-of-breeding-disputes temperament. No source checked for this guide reports unusual aggression specific to this species; treat it as a standard apisto, best kept in pairs or a male with multiple females rather than mixed male-to-male.

Breeding

Meinkeni is a cave spawner, but the two best records of its spawn size genuinely disagree. FishBase describes a typical clutch of 30–50 eggs attached to the ceiling of a cave, guarded solely by the female. A documented 2018 hobbyist breeding report (Don Kinyon, Potomac Valley Aquarium Society) logged real numbers well above that figure: roughly 150 eggs from the first female to spawn and more than 200 total between two pairs sharing a 20-gallon tank, laid on the underside of a clay pot. Eggs hatched in about two days, fry were free-swimming within five, and took microworms and newly-hatched brine shrimp immediately with no unusual mortality. Growth was modest — about 3/16" by 30 days, roughly double that by 60 days — though the keeper notes fry moved to other hobbyists grew somewhat faster, leaving open whether the slower rate reflected the species or simply his own water. His own reference books described the species as difficult to breed; his experience didn't bear that out, though he's candid that landing the right pair dynamics felt like "100% dumb luck" rather than a repeatable formula. One useful visual cue: the guarding female turns bright yellow. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

True Apistogramma meinkeni carries a documented, specific mislabeling risk, not just general trade confusion. A forum thread on apistogramma.com describes fish repeatedly sold as "A. meinkeni (wild)" that turned out, on closer inspection of tail shape against reference material, to actually be an undescribed form called A. sp. Erdfresser (also traded as A. sp. Porto Velho), collected from the Rio Purus near Porto Velho, Brazil — a river system entirely separate from meinkeni's own Rio Uaupés. German reference magazine Datz places this lookalike within the A. pertensis group this site already covers, while a different reference work, Cichlid Atlas 1, instead treats the Porto Velho fish as a synonym of A. pulchra — a call Datz itself disputes as a misidentification, leaving the form's status genuinely unresolved rather than settled either way. One longtime forum contributor reports personally encountering this exact mislabeled batch three separate times, each traced back to German wholesaler Aquarium Glaser roughly four weeks apart — the same wholesaler this site's own A. uaupesi page notes as a source for that similarly uncommon, same-region species. If you're buying meinkeni specifically, ask for collection-locality documentation and compare tail shape against reference photos before paying a premium for "wild" stock.

Final Thoughts

Meinkeni is a genuinely under-documented species in the English-language hobby literature, but what little record exists is unusually concrete: a named spawn log with real egg counts and growth rates, a specific and repeatedly-documented mislabeling problem traced to a named wholesaler, and a size record with an internal inconsistency worth flagging rather than smoothing over. It also carries one of the more charming naming stories on this site — honoring Hermann Meinken, a schoolteacher who became one of the hobby's own reference-literature founders. If you want a better-documented, more widely available Rio Negro-region relative to compare notes with, this site's own uaupesi shares general habitat and the same wholesale trade channel, though it's a visually distinct, separately-sourced species.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who was Apistogramma meinkeni named after?

Hermann Meinken (1896–1976), a German high-school biology teacher turned amateur ichthyologist who headed the V.D.A. (Association of German Aquarium and Terrarium Societies) fish identification office from 1949 until his death, and in 1951 co-founded the Commission for the Creation of German Fish Names. Between 1929 and 1975 he described 57 new cichlid, killifish, and cyprinid species — 24 remain valid today, including the popular Trigonostigma hengeli. He co-authored the standard German-language reference work known as "Holly–Meinken–Rachow" (Die Aquarienfische in Wort und Bild, 1934–1967), and in 1973, despite never completing a university degree, received an honorary doctorate from the University of Bremen on the strength of roughly 800 publications, formally receiving the Dr. rer. nat. title in November 1974. Sven O. Kullander named this species for him in his 1980 description, published in Bonner Zoologische Monographien No. 14.

How big does Apistogramma meinkeni get, and why do the size figures look unusual?

FishBase lists a maximum of 3.5cm standard length for males or unsexed fish and 4.5cm for females — notably, the female figure is larger than the male's, the reverse of the usual Apistogramma pattern where males grow larger and carry extended fins. A separate FishBase note gives a maximum of 6.0cm total length without specifying sex, which doesn't line up cleanly with either standard-length figure. No source checked for this guide resolves the inconsistency; treat the 3.5–4.5cm SL range as the more specific, citable figure and the 6.0cm TL note as a looser, less reliable upper bound.

Is Apistogramma meinkeni easy to breed, and how many eggs does a spawn produce?

Accounts disagree, which is itself informative. FishBase describes a typical spawn of 30–50 eggs attached to the ceiling of a cave, guarded by the female alone. A documented hobbyist breeding report (Don Kinyon, Potomac Valley Aquarium Society, 2018) logged a real spawn well above that: an estimated 150 eggs from one female and 200-plus total between two pairs in a single 20-gallon tank, using rainwater at pH 5.8 and about 80°F (27°C), with eggs laid on the underside of a clay pot. Eggs hatched in two days and fry were free-swimming by day five, taking microworms and newly-hatched brine shrimp immediately. The keeper's own reference books called the species difficult, but that wasn't borne out in practice — he describes the spawn itself as largely a matter of luck rather than a sign the species is especially hard to breed once conditions are right. A visible cue worth watching for: the guarding female turns bright yellow.

Why does Apistogramma meinkeni have a mislabeling problem in the trade?

A documented apistogramma.com forum thread describes fish repeatedly sold as "A. meinkeni (wild)" that were actually an undescribed form known as A. sp. Erdfresser (or A. sp. Porto Velho), collected from the Rio Purus at Porto Velho, Brazil — a different river system entirely from meinkeni's own Rio Uaupés. German reference magazine Datz places this lookalike in the A. pertensis group (see this site's own A. pertensis page), while a separate reference, Cichlid Atlas 1, treats the Porto Velho name as a synonym of A. pulchra — a treatment Datz itself disputes as a misidentification, leaving the form's exact status unresolved. A longtime forum contributor reports this was the third such mislabeled batch he had personally seen, all traced to German wholesaler Aquarium Glaser roughly four weeks apart. If you're sourcing true wild meinkeni, ask for collection-locality documentation rather than trusting the trade name alone.

What water parameters and habitat does Apistogramma meinkeni need?

FishBase lists its natural range as the Rio Uaupés basin — a Rio Negro tributary straddling the Brazil–Colombia border — with tropical freshwater conditions of pH 5.0–6.0, general hardness 6–10 dH, and temperature 22–29°C (72–84°F). A documented successful spawn used softer, more acidic rainwater at pH 5.8 and roughly 80°F (27°C), toward the gentler end of that general range. The IUCN rates the species Least Concern, assessed November 2018, so conservation status isn't a sourcing concern the way it is for several rarer species on this site.
