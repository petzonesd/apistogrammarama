Apistogramma resticulosa

Apistogramma resticulosa

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 12.8–26.5mm SL per type specimens; up to 3.9cm (1.5") per FishBase — one hobbyist source's 5.1–7.6cm claim does not match either and should be disregarded
  • Tank: 10–15g minimum per hobbyist sources (not scientifically documented)
  • pH: 6.3–7.5 (hobbyist sources; close to neutral, milder than genus blackwater specialists)
  • Temperature: 22–27.8°C (72–82°F) across hobbyist sources; 26°C in wild FishBase data
  • Hardness: 6–18°N/°d depending on source (hobbyist sources; not tightly agreed)
  • Temperament: Generally peaceful; reportedly more aggressive specifically while breeding
  • Difficulty: Advanced — genuinely rare in pure form, with a known hybrid/mislabeling trap in the trade

Overview

Apistogramma resticulosa is a small, little-documented dwarf cichlid from the Rio Madeira system in Brazil, described by Sven O. Kullander in 1980 — the same year as several other Kullander descriptions already on this site, including elizabethae, brevis, caetei, regani, and uaupesi, but in a separate, shorter paper of its own rather than Kullander's landmark Bonner Zoologische Monographien revision that produced most of those. The original description — "Description of a new species of Apistogramma from the Rio Madeira System in Brazil," Bulletin Zoologisch Museum, Universiteit van Amsterdam 7(16): 157–162 — was based on just six specimens and diagnosed the species by a vertical dash-marking pattern along the abdominal sides, which gave rise to both its Latin name (resticula, "small rope or cord") and its common hobby name, the Thin-line Dwarf Cichlid.

That same original description places resticulosa in the A. regani species-group — the same taxonomic cluster this site's own caetei, commbrae, pleurotaenia, regani, linkei, and gossei belong to. That makes resticulosa the seventh species on this site traced to that one group, spanning describers from Charles Tate Regan's original 1906–1909 work through Kullander's 1979–1986 run of revisions.

Natural Habitat

The type locality, per Kullander's original paper, is near Humaitá, a river town in Amazonas state, Brazil, sitting directly on the Rio Madeira — a major southern tributary of the Amazon. FishBase's habitat data describes the species as freshwater and benthopelagic in a tropical setting around 26°C, with no further wild-habitat chemistry reported beyond that single temperature figure. No source checked for this guide gives a fuller account of the specific stream conditions (blackwater vs. whitewater, substrate, vegetation) at the type locality, which is typical for a species described from just six museum specimens rather than extensively field-studied since.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: Not scientifically documented. Hobbyist sources suggest a 40–57 liter (10–15 US gallon) minimum, consistent with the genus-standard range used elsewhere on this site for similarly small species. See our tank setup guide.
  • Substrate: Fine sand is the hobbyist-source recommendation, letting the fish sift for food particles the way most Apistogramma do.
  • Décor: Driftwood, rocks, and dense planting to break sightlines, plus floating plants to diffuse light — the same low-light, broken-territory layout this site recommends genus-wide.
  • Water chemistry: Hobbyist sources disagree on the exact range — one gives pH 6.3–7.5 with 9–18°N hardness, another pH 7.0–7.5 with 6–10°d hardness — but both sit closer to neutral than this genus's extreme soft-acid blackwater specialists. See our water parameters guide.
  • Flow: Gentle current is the consistent hobbyist recommendation; not documented from wild data.

Feeding

Hobbyist sources describe resticulosa as an omnivore taking standard dwarf-cichlid fare: quality flake or small pellets as a staple, supplemented with live or frozen bloodworm, brine shrimp, mysis, and daphnia, fed in small portions a few times a day. No source checked for this guide documents feeding behavior from wild specimens or scientific study specifically. See our general feeding guide.

Temperament & Compatibility

Hobbyist sources describe resticulosa as peaceful toward other species and its own kind outside of breeding, with the usual caution against housing multiple males together. One source specifically flags increased aggression once a pair begins breeding. Suggested tankmates follow the genus-standard pattern used elsewhere on this site — calm, non-nippy fish such as small tetras and Corydoras that share similar water conditions. See our social dynamics guide for general stocking guidance.

Breeding

Resticulosa is a cave spawner, consistent with the rest of the genus, though no confirmed scientific breeding report turned up in the sources checked for this guide. Hobbyist accounts describe the standard apisto pattern: the female selects a cave or upturned small pot, lays and guards the eggs there, and the male defends the broader territory. Eggs are reported to hatch in roughly 4–5 days, with fry becoming free-swimming a few days after that and ready for newly hatched baby brine shrimp. One source recommends a 1-male-to-2-female ratio and notes breeding adults can become noticeably more aggressive than usual. Follow our general breeding guide for the standard conditioning-to-fry process until more rigorously documented species-specific detail exists.

Sourcing Notes

This is the species on this site with the clearest hybrid-mislabeling trap of all. A trade form sold as "Apistogramma sp. 'Steel Blue'" is widely believed to be a hybrid rather than a true species, and its suspected ancestry includes resticulosa alongside this site's own A. caetei and A. borellii — a three-way mixture, by the account checked for this guide. To compound the confusion, some exporters separately sell that same hybrid form mislabeled as A. agassizii. A pure, correctly identified resticulosa appears to be genuinely uncommon in retail channels; no established vendor listing for one turned up in the searches run for this guide, in contrast to "Steel Blue" itself, which is comparatively easy to find. If you're specifically after the true species rather than the hybrid trade form, ask any seller directly which one they're offering, and see our general sourcing guide for broader guidance on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Resticulosa is a quiet entry in the genus's taxonomic history — six museum specimens from near Humaitá on the Rio Madeira, described by Kullander in a short 1980 paper the same year as several of this site's bigger-name species, and placed by that same paper in the well-populated regani group alongside caetei, commbrae, pleurotaenia, regani, linkei, and gossei. Its bigger footprint in the modern hobby, ironically, may be as a namesake in someone else's hybrid: "Steel Blue" trades under a name close enough to cause real confusion. If you want the genuine article, go in with that mislabeling risk in mind, and expect the hunt itself to be most of the challenge.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where does Apistogramma resticulosa come from, and what does its name mean?

Sven O. Kullander described it in 1980 from six type specimens collected near Humaitá, Amazonas state, Brazil, in the Rio Madeira system. The original description (Bulletin Zoologisch Museum, Universiteit van Amsterdam 7(16): 157–162) diagnoses it by a vertical dash-marking pattern along the abdominal sides. That marking is almost certainly the source of both its scientific name — Latin resticula, a small rope or cord — and its common name, the Thin-line Dwarf Cichlid.

How big does Apistogramma resticulosa get?

Small, and sources genuinely conflict on exactly how small. Kullander's original type specimens measured 12.8–26.5mm standard length, and FishBase separately lists a maximum of 3.9cm (about 1.5in) SL for males or unsexed fish. One hobbyist reference instead claims 5.1–7.6cm (2–3in) — more than double the type-specimen and FishBase figures combined — which doesn't square with either source checked for this guide. Treat the smaller, museum-sourced figure as the reliable planning baseline.

What water parameters does Apistogramma resticulosa need?

FishBase's wild-habitat data gives a tropical environment around 26°C with no upper bound reported. Hobbyist sources diverge on the aquarium range: one gives pH 6.3–7.5 with 9–18°N hardness and 22–27°C, another gives a narrower pH 7.0–7.5 with 6–10°d hardness and 25.6–27.8°C. Both agree it tolerates a milder, closer-to-neutral range than this genus's extreme blackwater specialists. See our general water parameters guide.

Is Apistogramma resticulosa easy to breed?

No confirmed scientific breeding report was found for this guide, but hobbyist accounts describe standard apisto cave-spawning behavior: the female lays and guards eggs in a cave or upturned pot while the male defends the wider territory, with eggs hatching in roughly 4–5 days and fry becoming free-swimming a few days after that. One source recommends a 1-male-to-2-female ratio and notes the fish can turn aggressive specifically during breeding.

Is the fish sold as "Apistogramma sp. Steel Blue" actually Apistogramma resticulosa?

Not reliably, and this is the single biggest buying caution for this species. "Apistogramma sp. Steel Blue" is a trade form believed to be a hybrid whose ancestry may include resticulosa alongside this site's own A. caetei and A. borellii — and some exporters separately mislabel it as A. agassizii. A tag reading "resticulosa" or "Steel Blue" is not a guarantee of a pure, correctly identified fish; see our general sourcing guide before buying.
