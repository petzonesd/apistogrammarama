Apistogramma kullanderi

Apistogramma kullanderi

By Pet Zone SD Team • November 2026

The Essentials
  • Size: 3.1" (8.0cm SL) male, 2.3" (5.9cm SL) female — the largest known Apistogramma species
  • Tank: 20g minimum per commercial guidance; given its unusually large size for the genus, 29g+ is a safer default
  • pH: Not documented from wild habitat; commercial guidance is 6.0–7.0 (6.0–6.5 for breeding)
  • Temperature: Not documented from wild habitat; commercial guidance is 75–82°F (24–28°C)
  • Hardness: Not documented from wild habitat; ~5 dH for breeding per commercial guidance
  • Temperament: Not documented species-specifically; its much larger size warrants extra caution with smaller tankmates
  • Difficulty: Advanced/specialist — wholesale-only availability and a documented juvenile mislabeling risk

Overview

Apistogramma kullanderi is, by a wide margin, the largest Apistogramma species known to science. Henrique Varella and Mark Sabaj Pérez formally described it in 2014 in Ichthyological Explorations of Freshwaters (25(3):243–258), under a title that says it plainly: "A titan among dwarfs." The fish behind that title measured 79.7mm standard length — more than double the size of most of its genus, where many species top out under 50mm. Before the formal description, it had circulated informally among researchers as "Apistogramma gigas" and simply "Apistogramma sp.," collected on 29 October 2007 during an expedition to a remote Brazilian plateau.

The species name honors Sven O. Kullander, a Swedish ichthyologist whose decades of research on neotropical cichlids the describers credit as unmatched in the field. There's a direct connection to this site: Kullander isn't just being honored here — he's also the taxonomist who formally described this site's own A. hongsloi, back in 1979. It's a reversal of the pattern this site's A. erythrura profile documents with Wolfgang Staeck: there, a collector the hobby had already named a fish after came back decades later as a describer; here, a describer from decades ago has a brand-new species named in his honor.

Natural Habitat

Kullanderi is known only from the upper Río Curuá and two of its larger tributaries, on the Serra do Cachimbo plateau in Pará, Brazil, at roughly 500m elevation. The original description notes it was found exclusively above a series of three waterfalls (60m, 40m, and 10m) that separate the upper river from the lower Curuá — the species was absent below them in every site sampled. Specimens turned up in slow, shallow backwaters shaded by bankside vegetation, in water the original description characterizes as clear to mildly blackwater. Two other cichlids share this habitat: an undescribed, smaller Apistogramma species local to the same stretch of the upper Curuá, and Aequidens aff. rondoni. The describers speculate — explicitly as speculation, not a settled finding — that kullanderi's unusual size may trace to the absence of predators above the falls, female mate choice, or competition with the smaller co-occurring Apistogramma.

Tank Setup

  • Tank size: Commercial listings selling tank-bred stock recommend a 20-gallon minimum, with a larger tank preferred. Given that kullanderi runs roughly double the size of most other apistos on this site, this guide treats 29 gallons or more as the safer working default rather than the bare minimum. See our tank setup guide for scaling up further for a harem or breeding setup.
  • Substrate & décor: No wild-habitat-specific decor guidance exists in the sources checked for this guide. Commercial care sheets recommend a densely planted setup with plenty of hiding spots — consistent with genus-standard advice used elsewhere on this site.
  • Water chemistry: No pH, hardness, or temperature data from the wild habitat was published in the original description or found in any aquarium-specific source checked for this guide. Commercial guidance for tank-bred stock is pH 6.0–7.0 generally, narrowing to pH 6.0–6.5 at roughly 5 dH specifically for breeding. See our water parameters guide for building soft, slightly acidic water safely.
  • Filtration & flow: Not documented for this species specifically. Gentle flow and stable, mature water are the safe genus-wide default used elsewhere on this site — see our water quality guide.

Feeding

Commercial care guidance matches the genus-standard pattern used throughout this site: feed mainly sinking foods that reach the bottom third of the tank, such as sinking flakes and granules, as a daily base diet, with live or frozen foods like bloodworm and brine shrimp recommended specifically for conditioning a breeding pair. No source checked for this guide gives anything more species-specific, which is unsurprising for a fish this recently described and this limited in distribution. See our general feeding guide for broader conditioning strategy.

Temperament & Compatibility

No source checked for this guide rates kullanderi's temperament or aggression in detail, but its size is the one fact worth building a stocking plan around: at up to 8cm SL, an adult male is comfortably larger than most tankmates this site recommends for other apistos. Treat small, delicate species and fry from other tanks with more caution than the genus-standard advice elsewhere on this site assumes, and avoid housing it with the undescribed, smaller Apistogramma species it shares its native habitat with, given the real risk of confusing the two as juveniles (see Sourcing Notes below).

Breeding

Kullanderi is a cave spawner, consistent with every other species on this site. Commercial breeders recommend one male with several females when no bonded pair already exists, paired with a dedicated spawning surface and water nudged toward pH 6.0–6.5 at roughly 5 dH to help trigger spawning. Unlike this site's erythrura profile, which has no documented captive breeding report at all, German aquarium wholesaler Aquarium Glaser states that established breeding strains of true kullanderi already exist in the trade — a genuine, if limited, captive-breeding track record for a species described only in 2014. Follow our general breeding guide for the broader conditioning-to-fry process.

Sourcing Notes

Kullanderi comes with a documented, specific mislabeling risk, not just a generic caution. Aquarium Glaser — a wholesale importer that actually sells the species — states plainly that juvenile kullanderi closely resemble an undescribed Apistogramma species from the same stretch of the upper Río Curuá, and that young fish sold as kullanderi have sometimes turned out to be that other, unnamed species once they grew up. Because kullanderi is wholesale-only rather than a common pet-store fish, most buyers will be going through a specialist importer or breeder rather than a retail tank — ask specifically how an adult's identity was confirmed, not just how the juvenile looks. See our general sourcing guide for more on vetting a seller's claims.

Final Thoughts

Kullanderi is a genuine outlier on this site: the largest Apistogramma species known, from one of the most remote and recently-explored corners of the genus's range, with an actual captive-breeding track record despite being described only in 2014. It's not an easy or widely available fish — expect to source it through a specialist rather than a local store, and to double-check any juvenile's identity against the look-alike species it shares habitat with. What it offers in return is a direct link to this site's own hongsloi: the same Sven Kullander honored in this new species' name is the taxonomist who formally described hongsloi back in 1979, thirty-five years before this "titan among dwarfs" bore his name.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Apistogramma kullanderi called the "King of Dwarves"?

Because at roughly 8cm (79.7mm) standard length, the original description documents it as the largest Apistogramma species known — more than double the size of most of the genus, where many species top out under 5cm. Henrique Varella and Mark Sabaj Pérez formally described it in 2014 in Ichthyological Explorations of Freshwaters (25(3):243–258) under the title "A titan among dwarfs," from fish collected on 29 October 2007 during an expedition to Brazil's Serra do Cachimbo plateau. Before that, it circulated informally as "Apistogramma gigas" and simply "Apistogramma sp." The authors named it kullanderi to honor Sven O. Kullander, a Swedish ichthyologist whose decades of work on neotropical cichlids include formally describing this site's own A. hongsloi back in 1979 — the honoree of this new species already has his own handiwork on this site.

How big does Apistogramma kullanderi get, and is that confirmed?

FishBase, citing the original description, lists a maximum of 8.0cm (3.1in) standard length for males and 5.9cm (2.3in) for females — a wild-caught adult male measured 7.97cm SL. That female figure is itself larger than the average adult male of most other species on this site, which is the clearest way to grasp how unusual kullanderi's size is for this genus.

What water parameters does Apistogramma kullanderi need?

No primary wild-habitat water-chemistry data (pH, dH, or temperature) was published in the original description or found in any aquarium-specific source checked for this guide — only its habitat type (clear to mildly blackwater, slow shallow backwaters with bankside vegetation) is documented. Commercial listings selling tank-bred stock give pH 6.0–7.0 and 24–28°C (75–82°F) as general maintenance parameters, with breeding-tank guidance narrower still at pH 6.0–6.5 and roughly 5 dH. Treat the commercial figures as a working default rather than confirmed wild-parameter data. See our water parameters guide for building soft, slightly acidic water safely.

Is Apistogramma kullanderi endangered, and where does it come from?

FishBase lists its IUCN Red List status as Not Evaluated. It's known only from the upper Río Curuá and two larger tributaries on Brazil's Serra do Cachimbo plateau in Pará state, at roughly 500m elevation above three waterfalls (60m, 40m, and 10m) that isolate the population from the lower Curuá. That's a genuinely narrow, remote range, even though no formal conservation assessment has been made.

Is there a mislabeling risk when buying Apistogramma kullanderi?

Yes, and it's documented by at least one commercial importer. Aquarium Glaser notes that juvenile kullanderi look very similar to an undescribed Apistogramma species from the same upper Río Curuá area, and that young fish sold as kullanderi have sometimes turned out to be that other, still-unnamed species once they matured. Because kullanderi is a wholesale-only, specialist fish rather than a common pet-store item, ask any seller specifically how adult identity was confirmed rather than relying on a juvenile's appearance alone.
